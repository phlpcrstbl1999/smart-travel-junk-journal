Introduction & History

Stone Walls That Outlasted Typhoons

The Bato Church (Saint John the Baptist Parish) in Bato, Catanduanes is one of the oldest churches in the Bicol Region, originally established by Augustinian missionaries in the 17th century. Built from volcanic stone quarried from the island, the church was designed to withstand the powerful typhoons that regularly batter Catanduanes — and it has done exactly that for over three centuries.

The church's thick stone walls, buttressed facade, and solid bell tower reflect the Baroque colonial architecture typical of Spanish-era churches in the Philippines. Despite centuries of typhoons, earthquakes, and the passage of time, the structure remains remarkably intact and continues to serve as an active parish church for the community of Bato.

The church is surrounded by a well-maintained plaza and is a significant cultural and religious landmark for the people of Catanduanes. It is a testament to the craftsmanship of the island's early builders and the enduring faith of its community.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Bato Church

Bato Church is in Bato, Catanduanes — about 30 km south of Virac, the provincial capital. It is conveniently located along the main road connecting Virac to the southern municipalities.

01

Fly or Ferry to Catanduanes

Fly from Manila to Virac Airport (~1 hour), or take a bus to Tabaco City, Albay and board a ferry to San Andres, Catanduanes (~2.5₱3 hours). Virac is the main gateway to the island.

Flight: ~1 hr  |  Ferry: ~2.5₱3 hrs
02

Van or Jeepney to Bato

From Virac, take a van or jeepney bound for Bato (~45 minutes to 1 hour). The church is located in the town center of Bato, right along the main road — you can't miss it.

Van/Jeepney: ~₱40–₱60  |  — ~45 min₱1 hr
03

Explore the Church & Plaza

The Bato Church is open daily for visitors. Walk around the exterior to appreciate the thick stone walls and buttressed facade, then step inside to see the colonial-era interior. The adjacent plaza is a pleasant spot to rest.

Free entry  |  — Allow 30₱60 minutes
04

Combine with Puraran & Binurong

Bato is conveniently located between Virac and Baras, making it an easy stop on the way to or from Puraran Beach and Binurong Point. Plan a full-day circuit: Bato Church — Baras — Binurong Point — Puraran Beach — return to Virac.

Full-day circuit possible from Virac

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • March — June (Dry Season): Best weather for island travel. Roads are in good condition and the risk of typhoons is lowest.
  • Feast of St. John the Baptist (June 24): The church's patron feast day — a lively celebration with processions, music, and local festivities worth experiencing.
  • August — November: Coincides with surf season at Puraran. Combine the church visit with a surf trip to eastern Catanduanes.
  • Avoid peak typhoon months (SeptemberOctober) if possible — Catanduanes is the most typhoon-prone province in the Philippines.
—

Visiting the Church

  • Dress modestly — the church is an active place of worship. Cover shoulders and knees when entering the interior.
  • Mass schedules: Daily masses are held in the morning and evening. Check the schedule at the parish office to avoid disrupting services.
  • Photography: Exterior photography is freely permitted. Ask permission before photographing inside during services.
  • The bell tower is one of the most photogenic elements — best photographed in the morning light from the plaza.
📌

Planning Your Trip

  • Day trip from Virac: Bato is easily visited as a half-day trip from Virac, or as a stop on the way to Baras and Puraran Beach.
  • Rent a motorcycle in Virac for the most flexibility — you can visit Bato Church, Binurong Point, and Puraran Beach all in one day.
  • Local food in Bato: Try laing and fresh seafood at the local eateries near the church plaza.
  • Cash only — bring enough from Virac. ATM access outside the capital is limited.
Mini Itinerary

A Heritage & Nature Day in Catanduanes

Combine Bato Church with Binurong Point and Puraran Beach for a perfect day that blends colonial history with Catanduanes' wild natural beauty.

Morning
7:00 AM

Depart Virac

Start early from Virac by rented motorcycle or van. The road south to Bato passes through scenic coastal and mountain terrain — a beautiful drive in itself.

8:00 AM

Bato Church

Arrive at Bato Church in the morning light. Walk around the exterior, admire the thick volcanic stone walls and colonial facade, and step inside the church. The plaza is quiet and peaceful in the early morning.

9:30 AM

Continue to Baras

Continue east to Baras (~45 minutes from Bato). The road climbs through the island's mountainous interior with increasingly dramatic views as you approach the Pacific coast.

Afternoon
10:30 AM

Binurong Point

Trek to Binurong Point for the dramatic Pacific cliff views. Allow 2₱3 hours for the trek and exploration of the rock formations and tidal pools below.

1:30 PM

Lunch & Puraran Beach

Eat lunch in Baras, then head to Puraran Beach for the afternoon. Swim, watch the surfers, or take a beginner lesson on the legendary Majestic break.

5:00 PM

Return to Virac

Head back to Virac via the same route, passing Bato again at sunset. A full and deeply satisfying day on one of the Philippines' most underrated islands.
