Introduction & History

The Shrine of Valor

Located on the summit of Mount Samat in Pilar, Bataan, the Dambana ng Kagitingan (Shrine of Valor) is the Philippines' most important World War II memorial. The shrine was built to honor the Filipino and American soldiers who fought in the Battle of Bataan from January to April 1942 — one of the most significant and tragic battles of the Pacific War.

The centerpiece of the shrine is a massive 92-meter Latin cross — one of the tallest crosses in the world — visible from across Manila Bay on clear days. The cross contains an elevator that takes visitors to an observation deck near the top, offering a panoramic view of Bataan, Manila Bay, and on clear days, the distant skyline of Metro Manila. The arms of the cross contain a museum with exhibits about the Battle of Bataan and the infamous Bataan Death March.

The shrine was inaugurated on April 9, 1966 — the 24th anniversary of the Fall of Bataan — by President Ferdinand Marcos. April 9 is now observed as Araw ng Kagitingan (Day of Valor), a national holiday in the Philippines. The shrine grounds also contain a diorama museum, a memorial wall, and beautifully landscaped gardens overlooking the historic battlefield below.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Mount Samat

Mount Samat National Shrine is located in Pilar, Bataan — about 120 km west of Manila. It's one of the most accessible heritage destinations from the capital.

01

Bus from Manila to Balanga

Genesis Transport and Bataan Transit operate buses from Cubao or Pasay to Balanga City — the capital of Bataan. Travel time is about 2.5₱3 hours via the NLEX and SCTEX expressways.

Travel time: ~2.5₱3 hours  |  ~₱200–₱300 one-way
02

Balanga to Pilar

From Balanga, take a jeepney or van to Pilar municipality — about 20₱30 minutes. From Pilar town center, hire a tricycle or jeepney to the Mount Samat shrine entrance at the base of the mountain.

Jeepney to Pilar: ~20₱30 min  |  Tricycle to shrine: ~₱50–₱80
03

Drive or Take a Shuttle Up the Mountain

From the shrine entrance, you can drive up the mountain road (about 15₱20 minutes) or take a shuttle service. The road winds through forested slopes with views of Manila Bay opening up as you ascend.

Drive up: ~15₱20 min  |  Shuttle: ~₱50–₱100 per person
04

Arrive at the Shrine Summit

The shrine complex at the summit includes the cross, the diorama museum, the memorial wall, and the gardens. Entry is free. The elevator inside the cross takes visitors to the observation deck — a small fee applies.

🏛 Shrine entry: Free  |  Cross elevator: ~₱50  |  Open daily 8AM₱5PM

Essential Visitor Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): Best time — clear skies offer the most dramatic views of Manila Bay and the Bataan peninsula from the cross observation deck.
  • April 9 (Araw ng Kagitingan): The national Day of Valor — solemn ceremonies, military honors, and commemorative events at the shrine. A deeply moving experience.
  • Early Morning (8:00₱10:00 AM): The shrine is peaceful before tour groups arrive. Morning light on the cross and the bay views are spectacular.
  • Avoid weekends during peak season: The shrine can be crowded on weekends and holidays. Weekday visits are more peaceful and contemplative.
📌

What to See

  • The Cross: Take the elevator to the observation deck inside the 92-meter cross for panoramic views of Bataan, Manila Bay, and Metro Manila on clear days.
  • The Diorama Museum: Detailed dioramas depicting key scenes from the Battle of Bataan and the Bataan Death March. Essential historical context.
  • The Memorial Wall: A wall inscribed with the names of Filipino and American soldiers who fought in the Battle of Bataan.
  • The Gardens: Beautifully landscaped grounds with views of the battlefield below. A peaceful space for reflection.
📌

Etiquette & Respect

  • This is a war memorial. Approach it with solemnity and respect — many Filipino and American families have personal connections to this site.
  • Dress appropriately. Avoid beachwear or overly casual attire. The shrine is a sacred national memorial.
  • No loud music or disruptive behavior. The shrine is a place of remembrance — maintain a respectful atmosphere.
  • Photography is allowed throughout the grounds. Be respectful when photographing the memorial wall and the diorama museum.
Visitor Guide

How to Experience Mount Samat

A complete guide to the shrine and a suggested Bataan day tour combining the best of the province.

At the Shrine
Step 1

The Diorama Museum

Begin at the diorama museum at the base of the cross. The detailed scenes depicting the Battle of Bataan and the Death March provide essential historical context before ascending the cross. Allow 30₱45 minutes.

Step 2

The Memorial Wall

Walk along the memorial wall inscribed with the names of soldiers who fought in the Battle of Bataan. Take time to read the names and reflect on the scale of the sacrifice made here.

Step 3

The Cross — Observation Deck

Take the elevator inside the 92-meter cross to the observation deck. The panoramic view from the top — Manila Bay, the Bataan peninsula, and the distant Metro Manila skyline — is one of the most dramatic in Luzon.

Step 4

The Gardens & Reflection

Walk the shrine gardens and take in the view of the historic battlefield below. The combination of natural beauty and historical weight makes this one of the most powerful memorial sites in Southeast Asia.

Bataan Day Tour
Morning

Mount Samat National Shrine

Start your Bataan day at the shrine. Allow 2₱3 hours for the diorama museum, memorial wall, cross elevator, and gardens.

Midday

Las Casas Filipinas de Acuzar

Head to Las Casas in Bagac — a stunning heritage resort with restored Spanish colonial houses from across the Philippines. Guided tours available.

Afternoon

Five Fingers Cove

End your day at Five Fingers Cove — a dramatic coastal formation of five rocky peninsulas jutting into the South China Sea. One of the most scenic viewpoints in Bataan.
