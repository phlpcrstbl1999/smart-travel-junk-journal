Introduction & Overview

Where the Nation Gathers

Rizal Park (officially Luneta National Urban Park) is a 58-hectare public park in the heart of Manila, facing Manila Bay. It is the most historically significant public space in the Philippines · the site where national hero Dr. Jos Rizal was executed by firing squad on December 30, 1896, an event that ignited the Philippine Revolution against Spanish colonial rule.

The park's centerpiece is the Rizal Monument — a bronze statue of Rizal standing atop a granite obelisk, with his remains interred in the base. The monument is guarded 24 hours a day by the Philippine Army and is the site of the annual Rizal Day commemoration on December 30.

Beyond the monument, the park contains the Chinese Garden, Japanese Garden, Orchidarium, Planetarium, open-air concert area, and the famous Manila Bay sunset viewpoint — one of the most celebrated sunsets in Asia.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

Getting to Rizal Park

Rizal Park is in the heart of Manila along Roxas Boulevard — easily reached by LRT, jeepney, or taxi.

01

LRT Line 1 — United Nations Station

Take LRT Line 1 to United Nations Station. The park is a short walk south along Taft Avenue and Padre Burgos Avenue. The LRT is the fastest option from most parts of Manila.

LRT Line 1: UN Station  |  ~5 min walk
02

From Intramuros

Rizal Park is directly adjacent to Intramuros — walk south from the walled city to reach the park in about 10 minutes. Combine both in a single morning visit.

Walk from Intramuros: ~10 min  |  Combine both sites
03

Jeepney or Taxi

Jeepneys along Roxas Boulevard and Taft Avenue pass the park. Taxis and ride-hailing apps (Grab) are also convenient — tell the driver "Luneta" or "Rizal Park."

Taxi/Grab: ~₱80–₱150 from Ermita  |  Jeepney: ~₱12–₱15
04

Entrance & Layout

The park is free to enter. The main entrance is along Roxas Boulevard facing Manila Bay. The Rizal Monument is at the center, with the Chinese and Japanese Gardens to the east and the bay-facing promenade to the west.

Free admission  |  Best at sunrise or sunset

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Times to Visit

  • Sunrise (5:30₱7 AM): The park is at its most peaceful and beautiful at dawn — joggers, tai chi practitioners, and the golden light on the Rizal Monument.
  • Sunset: The Manila Bay sunset from the park's western promenade is one of the most celebrated in Asia — arrive by 5:30 PM for the best views.
  • December 30 (Rizal Day): The national holiday — solemn ceremonies at the Rizal Monument with military honors and wreath-laying.
  • Avoid Sunday afternoons: The park gets very crowded on Sunday afternoons with families and vendors.
📌

Park Highlights

  • Rizal Monument: The bronze statue of Rizal with his remains interred in the base — guarded 24 hours by the Philippine Army. The most sacred public monument in the Philippines.
  • Chinese Garden: A beautifully maintained traditional Chinese garden with pavilions, koi ponds, and a pagoda — a peaceful retreat within the park.
  • Japanese Garden: A serene Japanese-style garden with stone lanterns, a tea house, and a tranquil pond.
  • Manila Bay Promenade: The western edge of the park faces Manila Bay — the sunset here is legendary.
📌

Nearby Attractions

  • National Museum of the Philippines: Free admission — just across Padre Burgos Avenue from the park. Houses the Spoliarium by Juan Luna.
  • Intramuros: The Walled City is a 10-minute walk north — combine with Rizal Park for the complete historic Manila morning.
  • Manila Baywalk: The promenade along Roxas Boulevard extends south from the park — a pleasant evening walk with Manila Bay views.
  • Cultural Center of the Philippines: A short drive south along Roxas Boulevard — the premier arts venue in the country.
Mini Itinerary

Historic Manila in a Day

Combine Rizal Park with Intramuros, Fort Santiago, and the National Museum for the complete historic Manila experience.

Morning
6:00 AM

Sunrise at Rizal Park

Arrive at Rizal Park for sunrise. Walk to the Rizal Monument as the golden light hits the bronze statue. Watch the flag-raising ceremony and the changing of the guard.

8:00 AM

Intramuros & Fort Santiago

Walk north to Intramuros and spend the morning at Fort Santiago, San Agustin Church, and Manila Cathedral. The cobblestone streets are most atmospheric in the morning.

Afternoon
1:00 PM

National Museum of the Philippines

Spend the afternoon at the National Museum — free admission, world-class collections, and the iconic Spoliarium painting. Allow 2₱3 hours for a thorough visit.

5:30 PM

Manila Bay Sunset

Return to Rizal Park for the legendary Manila Bay sunset. The sky turns orange, pink, and purple over the bay — one of the most celebrated sunsets in Asia.
