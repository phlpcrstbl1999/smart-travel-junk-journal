Introduction & About

An Island at the Edge of the World

Cresta de Gallo — Spanish for "Rooster's Crest" — is a small, uninhabited island sitting alone in the Sibuyan Sea, administratively part of Romblon province. It is one of the most remote and pristine islands in the Philippines, and for those willing to make the journey, it delivers a beach experience that rivals anywhere in Southeast Asia.

The island is a narrow crescent of powdery white sand fringed by coconut palms, with crystal-clear water so shallow and calm that you can wade out 50 meters and still see the sandy bottom. The surrounding reef is healthy and teeming with marine life — sea turtles, reef sharks, and hundreds of fish species are regularly spotted by snorkelers and divers.

Because of its remoteness, Cresta de Gallo sees very few visitors. There are no permanent structures on the island — no resorts, no restaurants, no electricity. Visitors camp overnight or do a day trip by chartered boat. This is as close to a deserted island experience as you can get in the Philippines.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Cresta de Gallo

Cresta de Gallo is one of the most remote islands in the Philippines. Getting there requires a ferry to Romblon, then a chartered boat into the Sibuyan Sea.

01

Ferry from Batangas to Romblon

From Manila, take a bus to Batangas Port (~2₱3 hours), then an overnight RORO ferry to Romblon Port (~6₱8 hours). Montenegro Lines and other operators serve this route.

Bus: ~2₱3 hrs  |  Ferry: ~6₱8 hrs  |  ~₱550–₱950 total
02

Charter a Boat in Romblon

From Romblon town, arrange a chartered bangka to Cresta de Gallo. The boat ride takes approximately 2₱3 hours each way across the Sibuyan Sea. Book through your guesthouse or directly at the port — arrange the trip the day before.

Boat: ~2₱3 hrs each way  |  ~₱3,000₱5,000 for a full-day charter
03

Arrive at Cresta de Gallo

Your boat will anchor offshore and you'll wade or swim to the beach. There are no docking facilities. Bring everything you need for the day — food, water, snorkeling gear, and a tent if you plan to camp overnight.

Wade ashore  |  Camping permitted on the beach
04

Overnight Option

For the full experience, camp overnight on the island. Arrange with your boatman to return the next morning. Sleeping under the stars on a deserted island in the Sibuyan Sea is an experience you will never forget.

Overnight camping  |  Boatman returns next morning

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • March — May (Peak Season): Calmest seas and best visibility for snorkeling. The Sibuyan Sea can be rough — good weather is essential for the long boat ride.
  • November — February: Also good, but check weather forecasts carefully. The northeast monsoon can bring choppy seas.
  • Avoid June — October: Typhoon season makes the Sibuyan Sea crossing dangerous. Most boatmen will refuse to make the trip.
  • Check sea conditions the day before your trip. Your boatman will know if conditions are safe.
🎒

What to Pack

  • All food and water — there is nothing on the island. Bring more than you think you need, especially water.
  • Snorkeling gear — the reef around Cresta de Gallo is world-class. Don't miss it.
  • Tent and sleeping bag if camping overnight. The beach is flat and perfect for camping.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen — protect the pristine reef ecosystem.
  • Dry bags — everything gets wet during the wade ashore. Protect your electronics and valuables.
  • Cash — pay your boatman in cash. Bring extra for tips.
📌

Island & Safety Tips

  • Never go without a boatman. The Sibuyan Sea is open ocean — conditions can change quickly. Your boatman is your lifeline.
  • Leave no trace. Cresta de Gallo is pristine because visitors respect it. Pack out everything you bring in — every piece of trash.
  • Respect the reef. Do not stand on, touch, or collect coral. The reef is the island's greatest treasure.
  • Bring a first aid kit. The nearest medical facility is hours away. Be prepared for minor injuries.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend a Day at Cresta de Gallo

A day trip from Romblon gives you 4₱5 hours on the island. For the full experience, stay overnight and have the island entirely to yourself at sunrise.

Day Trip
5:30 AM

Depart Romblon by Boat

Leave Romblon port before sunrise for the 2₱3 hour crossing to Cresta de Gallo. The early departure ensures you arrive with maximum time on the island and avoids the afternoon winds that can make the return crossing rougher.

8:00 AM

Arrive at Cresta de Gallo

Wade ashore onto the white sand beach. Set up your camp or day base under the coconut palms. Take a moment to absorb the silence — no engines, no music, no other people. Just the sound of waves on a deserted island.

8:30 AM

Snorkeling the Reef

The reef around Cresta de Gallo is the main attraction. Snorkel along the drop-off on the eastern side of the island for the best marine life — sea turtles, reef sharks, giant clams, and hundreds of fish species in crystal-clear water.

Afternoon
11:00 AM

Beach Time & Lunch

Relax on the white sand, swim in the impossibly clear water, and eat your packed lunch under the shade of a coconut palm. This is the deserted island experience that most travelers only dream about.

1:00 PM

Explore the Island

Walk the full length of the island — it takes about 20 minutes end to end. The western side has calmer, shallower water; the eastern side has the best reef. The coconut grove in the center provides shade and a beautiful backdrop for photos.

2:30 PM

Depart for Romblon

Board your boat for the return crossing to Romblon. Arrive back in town by late afternoon, in time for dinner and a well-earned rest. If you stayed overnight, your boatman will collect you at the agreed time in the morning.
