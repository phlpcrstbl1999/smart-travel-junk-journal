Introduction & About

Romblon's Most Iconic Beach

Bonbon Beach sits on the northern coast of Romblon Island and is famous for its dramatic sandspit — a narrow strip of white sand that juts out into the turquoise sea, flanked by rocky outcrops and swaying coconut palms. At low tide, the sandbar extends fully, allowing visitors to walk out into the middle of the sea with water on both sides.

The beach is relatively undeveloped, which is a large part of its charm. There are no large resorts here — just a handful of small cottages, local vendors selling fresh coconuts, and the sound of waves. The water on both sides of the sandbar is calm and clear, perfect for swimming and snorkeling over the shallow reef.

Bonbon Beach is best experienced in the early morning, when the light is golden, the sandbar is fully exposed at low tide, and the crowds have yet to arrive. The view from the rocky headland above the beach — looking down at the curving white sandbar against the blue sea — is one of the most beautiful in the Philippines.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Bonbon Beach

Romblon Island is the capital island of Romblon province. Getting there requires a ferry from Batangas or a flight to Tugdan Airport on Tablas Island.

01

Bus to Batangas Port

From Manila (Cubao or Pasay), take a bus to Batangas Grand Terminal, then a tricycle to Batangas Port. Several bus lines operate this route. The ride takes about 2₱3 hours.

Bus: ~2₱3 hrs  |  ~₱150–₱250
02

Ferry from Batangas to Romblon

Take a RORO ferry from Batangas Port to Romblon Port. Montenegro Lines and other operators serve this route. The crossing takes about 6₱8 hours — overnight ferries are available.

Ferry: ~6₱8 hrs  |  ~₱400–₱700
03

Tricycle to Bonbon Beach

From Romblon town, hire a tricycle to Bonbon Beach on the northern coast of the island. The ride takes about 15₱20 minutes along a scenic coastal road. Ask your driver to wait or arrange a pick-up time.

Tricycle: ~15₱20 min  |  ~₱80–₱120
04

Alternatively: Fly to Tablas Island

Fly from Manila to Tugdan Airport on Tablas Island (~1 hour), then take a ferry or boat to Romblon Island (~1.5₱2 hours). This is faster but requires an additional boat transfer.

Flight: ~1 hr  |  Boat to Romblon: ~1.5₱2 hrs

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • Low Tide (6:00₱10:00 AM): The sandbar is fully exposed only at low tide. Check tide tables before you go — this is the defining experience of Bonbon Beach.
  • November — May (Dry Season): Calm seas, clear water, and reliable ferry schedules. Best overall conditions for Romblon.
  • Early Morning: Golden light, fewer visitors, and the sandbar at its most dramatic. Arrive by 7 AM for the best experience.
  • Avoid June — October: Typhoon season brings rough seas and possible ferry cancellations.
🎒

What to Pack

  • Tide table — check low tide times before visiting. The sandbar experience depends entirely on the tide.
  • Snorkeling gear — the reef around the sandbar has good marine life. Bring your own for the best experience.
  • Cash — Romblon has limited ATMs. Bring enough for your entire stay.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen — protect the coral reef and your skin.
  • Waterproof bag — for your phone and valuables during the boat ride and beach time.
📌

Beach Tips

  • Walk the full sandbar at low tide — the experience of standing on a narrow strip of sand with sea on both sides is unforgettable.
  • Climb the rocky headland above the beach for the best aerial-style view of the sandbar curving into the sea.
  • Combine with Romblon town — the marble workshops, Fort San Andres, and the cathedral are all within easy reach.
  • Pack out your trash. Bonbon Beach is pristine — help keep it that way.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend a Day in Romblon

Combine Bonbon Beach with Romblon town's heritage and marble culture for a full and rewarding island day.

Morning
6:30 AM

Bonbon Beach at Low Tide

Arrive at Bonbon Beach early to catch the sandbar at its fullest extent. Walk out to the tip of the sandbar, swim in the calm turquoise water on both sides, and snorkel over the shallow reef. The morning light on the white sand is spectacular.

9:00 AM

Headland Viewpoint

Climb the rocky headland at the end of the beach for a bird's-eye view of the sandbar and the surrounding coastline. This is the shot you've seen in every Romblon travel photo — and it's even better in person.

10:30 AM

Return to Romblon Town

Head back to Romblon town by tricycle. The town is compact and walkable — a charming provincial capital with a Spanish fort, a cathedral, and marble workshops lining the streets.

Afternoon
11:30 AM

Marble Workshop Visit

Visit one of Romblon's famous marble workshops — the province is known as the Marble Capital of the Philippines. Watch artisans carve statues, tiles, and decorative pieces from locally quarried marble. Buy a small piece as a unique pasalubong.

1:00 PM

Lunch & Fort San Andres

Eat lunch at a local restaurant in town, then visit Fort San Andres — a Spanish colonial fort built in 1640 overlooking the harbor. The view of Romblon Bay from the fort walls is one of the best in the province.

3:00 PM

Tiamban Beach Sunset

End the day at Tiamban Beach — a quieter beach near Romblon town with calm water and a beautiful sunset view. A perfect, peaceful close to a full day on the island.
