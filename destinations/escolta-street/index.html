Introduction & History

The Street That Was Manila's Fifth Avenue

Escolta Street in Binondo was the commercial heart of Manila from the Spanish colonial era through the American period — the most fashionable shopping street in Southeast Asia, lined with department stores, banks, hotels, and the finest establishments in the Philippines.

The street's golden age was the 1920s₱1940s, when magnificent Art Deco buildings were constructed along its length — the First United Building, the Regina Building, the El Hogar Filipino, and others that gave Escolta its distinctive architectural character. The 1945 Battle of Manila devastated much of the street, and subsequent decades of neglect left it a shadow of its former self.

Today, Escolta is experiencing a creative renaissance — young artists, designers, and entrepreneurs are restoring the Art Deco buildings and opening galleries, cafes, and creative spaces. The First United Building has been transformed into a hub for Manila's creative community, and the street is slowly reclaiming its identity as a center of culture and commerce.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

Getting to Escolta Street

Escolta Street is in Binondo, Manila — easily reached by LRT or on foot from Intramuros via Jones Bridge.

01

LRT Line 1 — Carriedo Station

Take LRT Line 1 to Carriedo Station. Walk north across the Quezon Bridge and turn left — Escolta Street is a short walk from the bridge.

LRT Line 1: Carriedo Station  |  ~10 min walk
02

From Intramuros via Jones Bridge

Walk north from Intramuros across Jones Bridge — Escolta Street begins just north of the bridge on the Binondo side of the Pasig River.

Walk from Intramuros: ~10 min  |  Via Jones Bridge
03

First United Building

The First United Building (1928) is the anchor of the Escolta revival — a beautifully restored Art Deco building now housing creative studios, galleries, and the popular Escolta Exchange market.

First United Building: 413 Escolta St  |  Creative hub
04

Combine with Binondo

Escolta is at the edge of Binondo — combine a walk down Escolta with a Binondo food tour for the complete old Manila experience.

Binondo: adjacent  |  Food tour available

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Art Deco Architecture

  • First United Building (1928): The finest Art Deco building on Escolta — now a creative hub with galleries, studios, and the Escolta Exchange market.
  • Regina Building: A beautifully preserved Art Deco facade — look up at the ornamental details above the ground floor shops.
  • El Hogar Filipino: A Spanish Renaissance building at the corner of Escolta and Jones Bridge — one of the most photographed heritage buildings in Manila.
  • Look up: The ground floors of Escolta's buildings are often occupied by ordinary shops — the architectural beauty is in the upper floors and facades.
📌

The Creative Revival

  • Escolta Exchange: A monthly market in the First United Building featuring local designers, artists, and food vendors — check the schedule before visiting.
  • Galleries and studios: Several creative spaces have opened in the restored buildings — look for signage and open doors.
  • Photography: Escolta is one of the most photogenic streets in Manila — the Art Deco facades, the street life, and the contrast between old and new make for compelling images.
  • Heritage walks: Several organizations offer guided heritage walks of Escolta and the surrounding Binondo area.
📌

Practical Tips

  • Best visited on weekdays: The street is most atmospheric on weekday mornings when the shops are open and the street life is active.
  • Combine with Binondo: Escolta is at the edge of Binondo — a Binondo food walk naturally includes Escolta.
  • Combine with Intramuros: Jones Bridge connects Escolta directly to Intramuros — visit both in a single morning.
  • The Pasig River: Walk to the Jones Bridge end of Escolta for views of the Pasig River and the Intramuros walls across the water.
Mini Itinerary

Old Manila Heritage Walk

Combine Escolta with Binondo, Intramuros, and the National Museum for the complete old Manila heritage experience.

Morning
8:00 AM

Escolta Street Walk

Start on Escolta Street — walk the length of the boulevard, photographing the Art Deco facades and exploring the First United Building. Have coffee at one of the new cafes in the restored buildings.

9:30 AM

Binondo Food Walk

Walk into Binondo for a food walk — hopia, goto, and dim sum from the world's oldest Chinatown. Visit Binondo Church and Ongpin Street.

11:00 AM

Intramuros via Jones Bridge

Walk across Jones Bridge to Intramuros — Fort Santiago, San Agustin Church, and Manila Cathedral.

Afternoon
2:00 PM

National Museum

Spend the afternoon at the National Museum of the Philippines — the Spoliarium, the Tree of Life, and the Balangay boats. Free admission.
