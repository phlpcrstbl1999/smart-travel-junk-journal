Introduction

Quezon's Most Sacred Pilgrimage Site

Kamay ni Hesus (Hands of Jesus) is the most visited pilgrimage site in Quezon province — a hilltop shrine in Lucban, Quezon that draws hundreds of thousands of pilgrims and visitors every year. The shrine is centered on a towering 50-foot statue of the Risen Christ at the summit of a hill, reached by climbing 305 life-size Stations of the Cross — concrete sculptures depicting the Passion of Christ carved into the hillside along the pilgrimage path.

The shrine was established by Father Joey Faller, a Franciscan priest who began the healing ministry here in the 1990s. The Kamay ni Hesus healing masses — held regularly at the shrine — have become one of the most attended religious events in the Philippines, drawing the sick, the faithful, and the curious from across the country. The atmosphere during healing masses is deeply moving — thousands of people gathered on the hillside, praying, singing, and seeking healing.

Beyond the religious significance, the shrine offers a genuinely beautiful hilltop experience — the climb through the Stations of the Cross is a meditative journey through lush Quezon forest, and the view from the summit, with the Risen Christ statue against the sky and the Quezon countryside spreading out below, is one of the most striking religious landscapes in the Philippines. The town of Lucban itself is famous for its longganisa (sausage) and the annual Pahiyas Festival — one of the most colorful harvest festivals in the country.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Kamay ni Hesus

Kamay ni Hesus is in Lucban, Quezon — about 130 km southeast of Manila. The drive takes about 2.5₱3 hours via SLEX and the Quezon highway.

01

Drive from Manila via SLEX

Drive via SLEX — Sto. Tomas — San Pablo — Lucban, Quezon. The shrine is well-signposted in Lucban. Having your own vehicle is ideal for combining with Villa Escudero (1 hour west) and exploring the Quezon countryside.

Manila to Lucban: ~2.5₱3 hours  |  Large parking area at the shrine
02

Bus from Manila to Lucban

Take a DLTB or JAC Liner bus from Buendia (Pasay) to Lucban, Quezon. Buses run regularly. From the Lucban terminal, take a tricycle to the Kamay ni Hesus shrine (about 10 minutes).

Manila to Lucban: ~3 hours  |  ~₱200–₱250  |  Tricycle: ~₱30
03

Climb the 305 Stations

The pilgrimage path climbs the hill through 305 life-size concrete sculptures depicting the Stations of the Cross — from the Last Supper to the Resurrection. The climb takes about 45 minutes to 1 hour at a meditative pace. The path is paved and well-maintained.

Climb: ~45 min₱1 hour  |  Free entrance  |  Wear comfortable shoes
04

Lucban Town & Longganisa

After the shrine, explore Lucban town — one of the most charming heritage towns in Quezon. Try the famous longganisang Lucban (Lucban-style sausage) from the market stalls, and visit the St. Louis Bishop Parish Church — a beautiful 17th-century colonial church on the town plaza.

Longganisang Lucban  |  — St. Louis Bishop Parish Church (1738)

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Visiting the Shrine

  • Open daily: The shrine is open every day from early morning to evening. Entrance is free. The healing masses are held on specific schedules — check the Kamay ni Hesus website or Facebook page for current mass schedules.
  • Dress modestly: The shrine is a sacred place of worship. Shoulders and knees must be covered. Avoid wearing shorts, sleeveless tops, or revealing clothing on the pilgrimage path.
  • Wear comfortable shoes: The 305-step climb is on a paved path but it is steep in sections. Wear closed-toe shoes with good grip. Flip-flops are not recommended for the full climb.
  • Holy Week: The shrine is at its most atmospheric during Holy Week — but also at its most crowded. Expect very large crowds on Good Friday and Black Saturday.
📌

The Healing Ministry

  • Healing masses: The Kamay ni Hesus healing masses are among the most attended religious events in the Philippines. Father Joey Faller's ministry has drawn millions of faithful seeking physical and spiritual healing.
  • The atmosphere: Even for non-religious visitors, the atmosphere during a healing mass is deeply moving — thousands of people gathered on the hillside in prayer and song is a powerful human experience.
  • Respect the faithful: Many visitors come to the shrine in genuine spiritual need. Be respectful of the religious atmosphere and the people around you, regardless of your own beliefs.
  • The Risen Christ statue: The 50-foot statue at the summit is the focal point of the shrine — the view from the base of the statue, looking out over the Quezon countryside, is magnificent.
📌

Lucban Town

  • Longganisang Lucban: Lucban is famous throughout the Philippines for its distinctive garlicky, vinegary sausage. Buy fresh longganisa from the market stalls near the town plaza — it's one of the best food souvenirs from Quezon.
  • Pahiyas Festival: Held every May 15 (feast of San Isidro Labrador), the Pahiyas Festival is one of the most colorful harvest festivals in the Philippines · houses are decorated with kiping (leaf-shaped rice wafers) and farm produce. Plan a visit around this date if possible.
  • St. Louis Bishop Parish Church: A beautiful 17th-century colonial church on the Lucban town plaza — founded in 1738 and one of the finest heritage churches in Quezon province.
  • Lucban pancit habhab: Try the local pancit habhab — noodles eaten directly from a banana leaf without utensils. A unique Lucban street food tradition.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend a Day in Lucban & Quezon

Combine Kamay ni Hesus with Villa Escudero for the ultimate Quezon day — plantation heritage in the morning, hilltop pilgrimage in the afternoon.

Morning
6:00 AM

Depart Manila

Leave Manila early for the 2.5₱3 hour drive to Lucban. Arriving by 9 AM gives you time to climb the Stations of the Cross before the midday heat and before the larger crowds arrive.

9:00 AM

Climb the 305 Stations

Begin the meditative climb through the 305 life-size Stations of the Cross. Take your time at each station — the sculptures are detailed and the forest setting is peaceful. The morning light through the trees is beautiful.

10:30 AM

Summit — The Risen Christ

Reach the summit and the 50-foot Risen Christ statue. The view from the top — the statue against the sky, the Quezon countryside below — is one of the most striking religious landscapes in the Philippines.

Afternoon
12:00 PM

Lucban Town Lunch

Descend and have lunch in Lucban town — try pancit habhab eaten from a banana leaf and longganisang Lucban. Visit the St. Louis Bishop Parish Church on the town plaza.

2:00 PM

Villa Escudero (Optional)

Drive 1 hour west to Villa Escudero for the afternoon plantation activities — carabao cart ride, bamboo rafting, and the folk arts museum. The waterfall restaurant serves lunch until 3 PM on some days — check in advance.

5:00 PM

Longganisa Shopping & Drive Home

Stock up on longganisang Lucban and pancit habhab from the market stalls before the drive back to Manila. The 3-hour drive is most comfortable in the early evening.
