Introduction & About

Stone That Built a Nation

Romblon province is known throughout the Philippines · and increasingly the world — as the Marble Capital of the Philippines. The island's mountains contain vast deposits of high-quality marble in a range of colors: pure white, grey-veined, green, black, and the prized Romblon white — considered among the finest marble in Asia.

Quarrying has been practiced here for over 400 years, and the craft of marble carving has been passed down through generations of Romblomanon families. Today, the province produces everything from floor tiles and countertops to religious statues, decorative figurines, and architectural elements that are exported across the Philippines and abroad.

Visiting the marble quarries and workshops is one of the most unique cultural experiences in MIMAROPA. You can watch quarrymen extract massive blocks of stone from the hillside, see craftsmen shape raw marble into finished pieces using hand tools and power grinders, and browse showrooms filled with everything from small souvenirs to full-sized sculptures.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Romblon

The marble quarries and workshops are located in and around Romblon town, the provincial capital on Romblon Island. Getting there requires a ferry from Batangas or a flight to Tablas Island.

01

Bus to Batangas Port

From Manila (Cubao or Pasay), take a bus to Batangas Grand Terminal, then a tricycle to Batangas Port. The ride takes about 2₱3 hours depending on traffic.

Bus: ~2₱3 hrs  |  ~₱150–₱250
02

Ferry from Batangas to Romblon

Take a RORO ferry from Batangas Port to Romblon Port. Montenegro Lines and other operators serve this route. The crossing takes about 6₱8 hours — overnight ferries are available and recommended.

Ferry: ~6₱8 hrs  |  ~₱400–₱700
03

Marble Workshops in Romblon Town

The marble workshops and showrooms are concentrated in Romblon town proper — many are within walking distance of the port. Ask locals to point you to the quarries, which are a short tricycle ride into the hills above town.

Walking distance from port  |  Tricycle to quarries: ~₱50–₱80
04

Alternatively: Fly to Tablas Island

Fly from Manila to Tugdan Airport on Tablas Island (~1 hour), then take a ferry or boat to Romblon Island (~1.5₱2 hours). This cuts total travel time significantly.

Flight: ~1 hr  |  Boat to Romblon: ~1.5₱2 hrs

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): Best time for Romblon overall — calm seas for the ferry crossing and good weather for quarry visits and beach trips.
  • Weekday mornings: Visit the quarries on a weekday morning to see the most activity — quarrymen at work, craftsmen carving, and workshops in full operation.
  • Avoid June — October: Typhoon season can disrupt ferry services and make the quarry roads muddy and difficult to access.
  • Year-round: The workshops and showrooms are open year-round — even during rainy season you can visit the indoor marble showrooms.
🎒

What to Pack & Buy

  • Extra luggage space — marble souvenirs are heavy. Plan ahead if you want to bring pieces home.
  • Cash — most marble workshops and quarries are cash-only. Bring enough for purchases and tips.
  • Closed-toe shoes — the quarry floor is rough and uneven. Sandals are not suitable for quarry visits.
  • Small marble pieces make excellent pasalubong — bookends, figurines, coasters, and small sculptures are affordable and unique.
📌

Quarry & Workshop Tips

  • Ask for a guided quarry tour. Local guides can take you into the active quarries and explain the extraction process — a fascinating industrial experience.
  • Watch the craftsmen work. Many workshops welcome visitors to watch the carving process. Don't be shy — the artisans are proud of their craft and happy to demonstrate.
  • Negotiate respectfully. Prices in showrooms are often negotiable, especially for larger pieces or multiple purchases.
  • Support local artisans by buying directly from family workshops rather than middlemen shops near the port.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend a Day with Romblon Marble

A full day in Romblon town lets you explore the quarries, watch craftsmen at work, visit heritage sites, and still make it to Bonbon Beach.

Morning
7:00 AM

Bonbon Beach at Low Tide

Start the day at Bonbon Beach to catch the sandbar at low tide. Swim, snorkel, and enjoy the beach before the heat of the day sets in. Return to town by 9:30 AM.

9:30 AM

Marble Quarry Visit

Take a tricycle up to the marble quarries in the hills above Romblon town. Watch quarrymen extract massive blocks of stone using diamond-wire saws and heavy machinery. The scale of the operation — and the beauty of the raw marble — is genuinely impressive.

11:00 AM

Marble Workshop & Showroom

Visit a family-run marble workshop in town. Watch craftsmen shape raw marble into finished pieces — from religious statues to decorative tiles. Browse the showroom and pick out your pasalubong.

Afternoon
1:00 PM

Lunch in Romblon Town

Eat at a local restaurant near the town plaza. Try fresh grilled fish, kinilaw (ceviche), and sinabawang isda — Romblon's seafood is exceptionally fresh. The town plaza is a pleasant spot to rest after the morning's activities.

2:30 PM

Fort San Andres & Cathedral

Visit Fort San Andres (1640) for panoramic views of the harbor, then walk to the Saint Joseph Cathedral — one of the oldest churches in MIMAROPA, with marble floors and altars sourced from local quarries.

4:30 PM

Sunset at the Harbor

Watch the sunset from the Romblon harbor promenade. The view of the bay, the surrounding islands, and the fishing boats returning at dusk is a perfect end to a full day in the Marble Capital of the Philippines.
