Introduction & About

The Philippines' Most Dramatic Coastline

The Caramoan Islands form a stunning archipelago off the tip of the Caramoan Peninsula in Camarines Sur, Bicol Region. The islands are characterized by dramatic limestone karst formations rising from turquoise water, hidden lagoons, white sand beaches, and coral reefs — a landscape so visually striking that it has been chosen as the filming location for multiple international editions of the Survivor reality series.

The main islands — Matukad, Lahos, Cotivas, Sabitang Laya, and others — are accessible by island hopping tours from the town of Caramoan. Each island has its own character: some have dramatic rock formations and hidden lagoons, others have long stretches of white sand backed by coconut palms, and several have excellent snorkeling over healthy coral reefs.

Despite its fame from Survivor, Caramoan remains relatively undeveloped and uncrowded compared to more famous Philippine beach destinations — making it one of the best value island hopping experiences in the country.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Caramoan Islands

Caramoan is at the tip of a peninsula in Camarines Sur. Getting there requires a bus to Naga or Sabang, then a boat or jeepney to Caramoan town.

01

Bus or Flight to Naga City

From Manila, take an overnight bus to Naga City (~8₱9 hours) or fly to Naga Airport (~1 hour). Naga is the main gateway to Caramoan.

Bus: ~8₱9 hrs  |  Flight: ~1 hr  |  ~₱600–₱3,000
02

Van to Sabang Port

From Naga, take a van to Sabang Port in Presentacion (~3 hours). This is the main jump-off point for the fast boat to Caramoan town.

Van: ~3 hrs  |  ~₱150–₱200
03

Fast Boat to Caramoan

From Sabang Port, take a fast boat to Caramoan town (~45 minutes). Boats run several times daily. Alternatively, take a jeepney via the mountain road (~4₱5 hours).

Fast boat: ~45 min  |  ~₱200–₱250
04

Island Hopping from Caramoan

Book an island hopping tour from Caramoan town port. Tours typically cover 4₱6 islands in a full day. Book through your guesthouse or at the port the evening before.

Island hopping: ~₱1,500₱2,500/boat  |  — Full day

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • March — June (Dry Season): Best conditions — calm seas, clear water, and reliable island hopping. Peak season is AprilMay.
  • November — February: Also good but occasional northeast monsoon swells. Check weather before booking.
  • Avoid July — October: Typhoon season — rough seas and possible tour cancellations.
  • Weekdays: Far fewer visitors — the islands feel genuinely deserted on weekdays.
🎒

What to Pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen — protect Caramoan's healthy coral reefs.
  • Snorkeling gear — bring your own for the best experience.
  • Cash — ATMs are limited in Caramoan town. Bring enough from Naga.
  • Waterproof bag — for your phone and valuables on the boat.
  • Light jacket — evenings can be breezy on the peninsula.
🚤

Island Hopping Tips

  • Hire a dedicated boat rather than joining a group tour for more flexibility on timing and island selection.
  • Must-visit islands: Matukad (hidden lagoon), Lahos (white sand), Sabitang Laya (sandbar), Cotivas (snorkeling).
  • Start early — depart by 8 AM to maximize time on the islands before afternoon winds pick up.
  • Pack your own food and water — options on the islands are very limited.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend 2 Days in Caramoan

Two days gives you time for the best island hopping and a chance to explore the peninsula's beaches and caves.

Day 1 — Island Hopping
8:00 AM

Matukad Island

Start at Matukad Island — climb the rocky path to the hidden lagoon above the beach. The view of the turquoise lagoon enclosed by limestone walls is one of Caramoan's most dramatic sights.

10:30 AM

Lahos Island & Snorkeling

Head to Lahos Island for its long white sand beach and excellent snorkeling over the surrounding reef. Lunch on the beach — your boatman will prepare fresh grilled fish.

2:00 PM

Sabitang Laya Sandbar

Visit Sabitang Laya — a narrow sandbar island that appears and disappears with the tide. Walk the full length with water on both sides for the classic Caramoan photo.

Day 2 — More Islands
8:00 AM

Cotivas Island

Cotivas Island has some of the best snorkeling in Caramoan — diverse coral, abundant fish, and clear water. Spend the morning exploring the reef.

11:00 AM

Gota Beach

Visit Gota Beach — the most famous beach on the peninsula, with fine white sand and dramatic limestone cliffs. This was one of the main Survivor filming locations.

3:00 PM

Return to Caramoan Town

Return to town, freshen up, and prepare for your journey back to Naga. Try the local seafood at a restaurant near the port before departing.
