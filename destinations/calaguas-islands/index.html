Introduction & About

Off the Grid, Off the Charts

The Calaguas Islands — a group of small islands in the Lamon Bay off the coast of Vinzons, Camarines Norte — have become one of the most talked-about beach destinations in the Philippines among travelers seeking something genuinely unspoiled. The main beach, Mahabang Buhangin (Long Beach), stretches for over a kilometer of fine white sand with clear turquoise water and no permanent structures in sight.

There is no electricity on the islands, no resorts, and no running water. Visitors camp on the beach in tents, cook over open fires, and fall asleep to the sound of waves under a sky full of stars. It is one of the last places near Manila where you can experience a truly raw, undeveloped island — and that is precisely what makes it extraordinary.

The islands are best reached from Daet, the capital of Camarines Norte, via a boat from the port of Paracale or Vinzons. The journey takes 2₱3 hours by bangka across open water — part of the adventure that filters out casual visitors and keeps the islands pristine.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Calaguas Islands

Calaguas is reached via Daet, Camarines Norte — about 330 km from Manila. The journey combines a bus ride and a boat crossing.

01

Bus from Manila to Daet

Take an overnight bus from Manila (Cubao or Pasay) to Daet, Camarines Norte. Philtranco, DLTB Co., and other operators serve this route. The journey takes about 7₱8 hours. Book a deluxe seat for comfort.

Bus: ~7₱8 hrs  |  ~₱500–₱800 one-way
02

Tricycle to the Boat Port

From Daet, take a tricycle to the jump-off port — either Paracale Port or Vinzons Port, depending on your tour operator. Most organized tours arrange transport from Daet directly to the port.

Tricycle: ~30₱45 min  |  ~₱100–₱150
03

Bangka to Calaguas

Board a bangka (outrigger boat) for the 2₱3 hour crossing to Calaguas Islands. The open-water crossing can be rough — bring motion sickness medication if needed. The arrival at Mahabang Buhangin makes every minute worthwhile.

Bangka: ~2₱3 hrs  |  ~₱500–₱800 per person (shared boat)
04

Book a Tour Package

The easiest way to visit Calaguas is through a tour package from Daet or Manila. Packages typically include boat transfers, tent rental, meals, and a guide. Highly recommended for first-time visitors — the logistics are complex without local knowledge.

2D1N package: ~₱1,500₱2,500 per person  |  Camping included

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • March — May (Peak Season): Calmest seas and best weather. The crossing is safest and the beach is at its most beautiful. Book well in advance — Calaguas fills up fast during summer.
  • November — February: Also good, but the northeast monsoon can bring choppy seas. Check weather forecasts before booking.
  • Avoid June — October: Typhoon season makes the open-water crossing dangerous. Most boat operators suspend trips during this period.
  • Weekdays: Far fewer visitors — the beach feels genuinely deserted on weekdays even during peak season.
📌

Camping Essentials

  • Tent — bring your own or rent from your tour operator. There are no permanent structures on the island.
  • All food and water — there are no stores or restaurants. Bring everything you need, including extra drinking water.
  • Portable charger / power bank — there is no electricity. Charge all devices fully before leaving Daet.
  • Flashlight or headlamp — nights on the island are completely dark. Essential for moving around after sunset.
  • Cash only — no ATMs, no signal, no card payments anywhere near the island.
📌

Island & Sea Tips

  • Reef-safe sunscreen only — the waters around Calaguas have healthy coral. Regular sunscreen is harmful to the reef ecosystem.
  • Pack out all trash. Calaguas is pristine because visitors respect it. Leave absolutely nothing behind.
  • Respect the local community. Calaguas has a small fishing community — be respectful of their space and livelihood.
  • Motion sickness medication — the open-water crossing can be rough. Take medication before boarding the boat.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend a Weekend at Calaguas

A 2-day, 1-night trip is the standard Calaguas experience — enough time to fully enjoy the beach, explore the island, and sleep under the stars.

Day 1
5:00 AM

Depart Daet by Boat

Board your bangka at the port before sunrise for the 2₱3 hour crossing to Calaguas. The early departure ensures you arrive with a full day on the island. Bring a jacket — the open-water crossing is cool and breezy in the early morning.

8:00 AM

Arrive at Mahabang Buhangin

Step onto Mahabang Buhangin — over a kilometer of fine white sand with no structures, no vendors, and no noise except the waves. Set up your tent under the coconut palms and take a moment to absorb the silence.

9:00 AM — 5:00 PM

Beach Day

Swim in the clear water, snorkel over the reef, explore the length of the beach, and simply relax. There is nothing to do here except enjoy the island — and that is exactly the point. Watch the sunset from the beach as the sky turns orange and pink over the sea.

Day 2
5:30 AM

Sunrise on the Beach

Wake up before sunrise and walk to the water's edge. The sunrise over the sea from Calaguas — with no light pollution, no buildings, and no sound except the waves — is one of the most peaceful and beautiful experiences in Philippine travel.

8:00 AM

Island Exploration

Explore the other parts of the island — the rocky headlands at each end of the beach, the small fishing community, and the interior coconut groves. The island is small enough to walk end to end in under an hour.

10:00 AM

Return to Daet

Pack up camp and board the bangka for the return crossing to the mainland. From Daet, take a bus back to Manila or continue south to explore more of the Bicol Region — Bagasbas Beach is just minutes away.
