Introduction & Overview

Luzon's Highest Peak

Mt. Pulag (2,922m) is the highest mountain in Luzon and the third highest in the Philippines. Located in Benguet province in the Cordillera Administrative Region, it is a protected national park and one of the most biodiverse mountain ecosystems in the country.

The mountain is sacred to the indigenous Ibaloi, Kankanaey, and Kalanguya peoples who have lived on its slopes for centuries. They believe the mountain is the dwelling place of their ancestors' spirits, and trekkers are expected to approach it with respect.

The main attraction is the sea of clouds — a breathtaking phenomenon where clouds fill the valleys below the summit at dawn, creating the illusion of floating above a white ocean. The summit trail passes through unique dwarf bamboo grasslands and mossy forest ecosystems found nowhere else in Luzon. Temperatures at the summit can drop to near freezing at night.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Mt. Pulag

Mt. Pulag is accessed via Kabayan, Benguet — the jump-off point for the Ambangeg Trail (most popular) and other routes. Registration is mandatory at the DENR office.

01

Bus to Baguio City

Take a bus from Manila to Baguio City (~5₱6 hours). Baguio is the main staging point for Mt. Pulag treks. Most trekkers spend a night in Baguio before heading to the mountain.

Bus: ~5₱6 hrs  |  ~₱400–₱600
02

Van to Kabayan or Ambangeg

From Baguio, take a van to Kabayan (~3₱4 hours) or directly to the Ambangeg Ranger Station (~2.5 hours). The Ambangeg Trail is the most popular and accessible route to the summit.

Van: ~₱150–₱250  |  — ~2.5₱4 hrs
03

Register at DENR Ranger Station

All trekkers must register at the DENR Ranger Station and pay the entrance and environmental fees. A licensed guide is mandatory for all routes. Book your guide in advance through accredited tour operators.

Fees: ~₱200–₱500  |  Guide: mandatory
04

Trek to the Summit

The Ambangeg Trail takes 3₱4 hours to the summit. Most trekkers do an overnight camp to wake before dawn for the sea of clouds. The trail passes through pine forest, mossy forest, and the iconic dwarf bamboo grasslands near the summit.

Ambangeg Trail: ~3₱4 hrs  |  Overnight camping recommended

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Time for Sea of Clouds

  • October — February (Dry Season): Best chance of clear skies and the sea of clouds phenomenon. The summit is coldest during these months — temperatures can drop to 0₱5°C at night.
  • March — May: Still good conditions but the sea of clouds is less reliable as the dry season ends.
  • June — September (Rainy Season): The mountain is often clouded over and trails can be slippery. Not recommended for first-time trekkers.
  • Weekdays are less crowded: Mt. Pulag gets very busy on weekends. Trek on a weekday for a more peaceful experience.
🧦

Trekking Tips

  • Physical fitness required: The Ambangeg Trail is rated moderate — you should be able to walk 6₱8 hours with a pack. Train beforehand if you're not a regular hiker.
  • Layers are essential: Temperatures drop dramatically at the summit. Bring a thermal base layer, fleece, and a waterproof outer layer.
  • Leave No Trace: Mt. Pulag is a protected national park and a sacred indigenous site. Carry out all waste and respect the environment.
  • Acclimatize in Baguio: Spend a night in Baguio (1,500m) before trekking to help your body adjust to the altitude.
🎒

What to Pack

  • Warm sleeping bag rated to at least 5°C — summit nights are cold and your sleeping bag is your most important piece of gear.
  • Headlamp with extra batteries — you'll be waking at 3₱4 AM to hike to the summit for sunrise.
  • Trekking poles — helpful on the steep sections of the trail, especially on the descent.
  • High-energy snacks and water — bring enough food and at least 2 liters of water per person per day.
Mini Itinerary

2 Days on Mt. Pulag

The classic Mt. Pulag overnight trek — arrive at the ranger station in the afternoon, camp near the summit, and wake before dawn for the sea of clouds.

Day 1
Afternoon

Arrive at Ranger Station

Arrive at the Ambangeg Ranger Station in the early afternoon. Register, pay fees, meet your guide, and pack your gear. Begin the trek in the late afternoon to reach the campsite before dark.

Late Afternoon

Trek to Camp

Trek through pine forest and mossy forest to the designated campsite (~2₱3 hours). Set up camp, cook dinner, and rest early — you'll be waking at 3 AM.

Evening

Stargazing at the Summit

If skies are clear, the summit area offers extraordinary stargazing — at 2,900m with minimal light pollution, the Milky Way is visible on clear nights.

Day 2
3:30 AM

Summit Push for Sunrise

Wake before dawn and trek to the summit (~1 hour from camp). Arrive before sunrise and wait for the sea of clouds to form as the sun rises over the Cordillera mountains. One of the most extraordinary natural spectacles in the Philippines.

Morning

Descent

After sunrise, descend back to camp, pack up, and trek down to the ranger station. The descent takes 2₱3 hours. Return to Baguio for a well-earned meal and rest.
