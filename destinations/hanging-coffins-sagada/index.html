Introduction & History

An Ancient Burial Tradition

The Hanging Coffins of Sagada are one of the most extraordinary cultural sites in the Philippines · ancient wooden coffins nailed to the limestone cliffs of Echo Valley in Sagada, Mountain Province. The practice of hanging coffins on cliff faces is a tradition of the Igorot people of Sagada, believed to date back at least 2,000 years.

According to Igorot belief, placing the dead high on the cliffs brings them closer to the spirit world and allows them to watch over their living descendants in the valley below. The coffins are carved from hollowed logs, and the deceased are placed inside in a fetal position — symbolizing the return to the womb and the cycle of life.

The hanging coffins are reached via a short trek through Echo Valley — a dramatic limestone gorge where the cliffs rise steeply on both sides. The valley is named for its remarkable acoustics — sounds echo dramatically off the limestone walls. The combination of the ancient coffins, the dramatic cliffs, and the mountain forest creates an atmosphere of profound solemnity and beauty.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to the Hanging Coffins

The Hanging Coffins are in Sagada, Mountain Province — reached via Baguio City and a scenic mountain road through the Cordillera.

01

Bus to Baguio City

Take a bus from Manila to Baguio City (~5₱6 hours). Baguio is the main staging point for Sagada trips.

Bus: ~5₱6 hrs  |  ~₱400–₱600
02

Bus from Baguio to Sagada

From Baguio City, take a bus or van to Sagada (~5₱6 hours). The road passes through Bontoc, the capital of Mountain Province. The scenery along the Halsema Highway is spectacular.

Bus/van: ~5₱6 hrs  |  ~₱200–₱350
03

Register at the Sagada Tourism Office

All visitors to Sagada must register at the Sagada Tourism Office and pay the environmental fee. A licensed local guide is mandatory for visiting the Hanging Coffins and Sumaguing Cave.

Environmental fee: ~₱50–₱100  |  Guide: mandatory
04

Trek to Echo Valley

From the Sagada town center, a 30-minute trek leads through pine forest to Echo Valley and the Hanging Coffins viewpoint. The trail is well-marked and the guide will explain the cultural significance of the burial tradition.

Trek: ~30 min  |  Guide fee: ~₱300–₱500

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • March — May (Dry Season): Best weather for the Echo Valley trek. Clear skies and dry trails.
  • November — February: Cool and misty — the fog in Echo Valley adds an atmospheric quality to the hanging coffins.
  • Avoid peak season crowds: Sagada gets very busy during Holy Week and long weekends. Visit on weekdays for a more peaceful experience.
  • Morning visits: The light in Echo Valley is best in the morning. Arrive early before the afternoon crowds.
📌

Cultural Respect

  • The coffins are sacred: Do not touch, climb on, or disturb the coffins in any way. They are the resting places of real people and are deeply sacred to the Sagada community.
  • Speak quietly: Echo Valley is a place of the dead. Maintain a respectful tone and volume throughout the visit.
  • Photography: Photography is permitted but should be done respectfully. Do not use flash near the coffins.
  • Follow your guide: The guide will explain the cultural protocols and ensure you visit the site with appropriate respect.
📌

Sagada Highlights

  • Sumaguing Cave: The most spectacular cave in Sagada — a massive limestone cavern with dramatic formations. Combine with the Hanging Coffins for the complete Sagada experience.
  • Bomod-ok Falls: A beautiful 200-foot waterfall about 2 hours from Sagada town — a rewarding half-day trek through rice terraces and forest.
  • Sagada sunrise: The view from the Sagada viewpoint at sunrise — with the mountains and valleys below — is one of the most beautiful in the Cordillera.
  • Sagada coffee: The local Arabica coffee grown in the Sagada highlands is exceptional. Try it at a local cafe.
Mini Itinerary

2 Days in Sagada

Combine the Hanging Coffins with Sumaguing Cave and Bomod-ok Falls for the complete Sagada experience.

Day 1
Morning

Arrive & Register

Arrive in Sagada and register at the tourism office. Have breakfast at a local cafe — try the Sagada Arabica coffee and local Igorot dishes.

Mid-Morning

Hanging Coffins of Echo Valley

Trek to Echo Valley with your guide. Stand before the ancient hanging coffins on the limestone cliffs and listen to your guide explain the 2,000-year-old burial tradition of the Igorot people.

Afternoon

Sumaguing Cave

Spend the afternoon exploring Sumaguing Cave — the most spectacular cave in Sagada, with massive chambers, dramatic limestone formations, and an underground river.

Day 2
Dawn

Sagada Sunrise

Wake before dawn for the Sagada sunrise from the viewpoint above town. The mountains and valleys below turn gold as the sun rises over the Cordillera.

Morning

Bomod-ok Falls

Trek to Bomod-ok Falls — a 2-hour walk through rice terraces and forest to a beautiful 200-foot waterfall. Swim in the natural pool at the base.

Afternoon

Return to Baguio

Take the afternoon bus back to Baguio City or continue to the next Cordillera destination. Buy Sagada coffee and local crafts as pasalubong.
