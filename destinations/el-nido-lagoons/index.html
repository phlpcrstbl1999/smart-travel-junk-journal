Introduction & About

The Lagoons That Define Palawan

El Nido, in the northern tip of Palawan, is home to one of the most spectacular seascapes in the world — a maze of 45+ limestone karst islands and islets rising dramatically from the Sulu Sea, enclosing hidden lagoons, secret beaches, and pristine coral reefs within the Bacuit Archipelago.

The most famous of El Nido's lagoons are on Miniloc Island: the Big Lagoon — a vast, calm body of emerald water surrounded by sheer limestone walls — and the Small Lagoon, accessible only by swimming or kayaking through a narrow opening in the rock. Both are part of the iconic Tour A island hopping route, considered one of the best day trips in Southeast Asia.

Beyond the lagoons, El Nido offers Secret Beach (entered through an underwater cave), Seven Commandos Beach, the Cathedral Cave, and dozens of snorkeling spots over healthy coral reefs. The entire area is part of the El Nido-Taytay Managed Resource Protected Area, a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to El Nido

El Nido is at the northern tip of Palawan, about 240 km from Puerto Princesa. It's reachable by direct flight from Manila or by a combination of flight and overland travel.

01

Fly Direct to El Nido

The fastest option — fly from Manila to Lio Airport in El Nido. AirSWIFT operates this route. The flight takes about 1 hour 20 minutes and drops you directly in El Nido town, a short tricycle ride from the beach.

Flight: ~1 hr 20 min  |  ~₱3,000–₱8,000 one-way
02

Fly to Puerto Princesa, then Van to El Nido

Fly from Manila to Puerto Princesa (~1 hr 20 min), then take a van or bus to El Nido (~5₱6 hours). This is cheaper but longer. The van ride through Palawan's interior is scenic and worth doing at least once.

Flight: ~1 hr 20 min  |  Van: ~5₱6 hrs  |  ~₱2,500–₱5,000 total
03

Book Your Island Hopping Tour

Island hopping tours depart daily from El Nido town beach. Tour A covers the Big Lagoon, Small Lagoon, Secret Beach, and Shimizu Island — the most popular route. Book through your guesthouse or at the beach the evening before.

Tour A: ~₱1,200–₱1,500 per person  |  — Full day
04

Environmental Fee

All visitors to El Nido must pay an Environmental User Fee (EUF) of ₱200 per person, valid for 10 days. This is collected at the El Nido Tourism Office or at the beach before boarding your tour boat. Keep your receipt.

EUF: ₱200 per person  |  — Valid for 10 days

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): Best conditions — calm seas, clear water, and reliable island hopping. Peak season is DecemberApril.
  • December — February: Busiest period. Book accommodation and tours well in advance. Prices are highest.
  • June — October: Rainy season brings rough seas and some tour cancellations, but fewer crowds and lower prices. Some days are still beautiful.
  • Shoulder season (May, November): Best value — good weather with fewer tourists and lower prices.
🎒

What to Pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen — mandatory for protecting El Nido's coral reefs. Regular sunscreen is banned in the protected area.
  • Snorkeling gear — bring your own mask and fins for the best experience. Rentals are available but quality varies.
  • Waterproof phone case — you will get wet on the island hopping boats.
  • Cash — ATMs in El Nido are limited and often run out. Bring enough from Puerto Princesa or Manila.
  • Light layers — evenings in El Nido can be breezy. A light jacket is useful.
🚤

Island Hopping Tips

  • Do Tour A first. The Big Lagoon and Small Lagoon are the highlights of El Nido — don't leave without seeing them.
  • Kayak the Small Lagoon. Kayaks are available at the entrance — paddling through the narrow opening into the enclosed lagoon is one of the best experiences in Palawan.
  • Start early. Tours depart at 9 AM — arrive at the beach by 8:30 AM to secure a good spot on the boat.
  • Respect the no-entry zones. Some lagoons have restricted access to protect the ecosystem. Follow your guide's instructions.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend 2 Days in El Nido

Two days lets you cover the best of El Nido — Tour A for the lagoons, Tour C for the beaches, and time to explore the town at night.

Day 1 — Tour A
9:00 AM

Big Lagoon, Miniloc Island

Your first stop — the Big Lagoon. Paddle a kayak or swim into the vast emerald lagoon enclosed by sheer limestone walls. The scale and color of the water are unlike anything you've seen. Spend at least an hour here.

11:00 AM

Small Lagoon

Swim or kayak through the narrow rock opening into the Small Lagoon — a perfectly enclosed body of turquoise water surrounded by towering limestone. The passage through the rock is tight but the reward on the other side is extraordinary.

1:00 PM

Lunch on the Beach + Secret Beach

Your boatman prepares a fresh seafood lunch on a secluded beach. After eating, swim through an underwater cave entrance to reach Secret Beach — a hidden cove completely enclosed by limestone, accessible only by diving under the rock.

Day 2 — Tour C
9:00 AM

Hidden Beach & Matinloc Shrine

Tour C covers the northern islands. Start at Hidden Beach — a secluded white sand cove accessible only through a narrow gap in the limestone. Then visit the Matinloc Shrine, a hilltop chapel with panoramic views of the archipelago.

11:30 AM

Tapiutan Strait Snorkeling

Snorkel the Tapiutan Strait — one of El Nido's best reef sites with excellent visibility, diverse coral, and abundant fish life. Sea turtles are frequently spotted here.

3:00 PM

Sunset at El Nido Town Beach

Return to El Nido town and watch the sunset from the main beach. The silhouette of the limestone karsts against the orange sky is one of the most iconic images in Philippine travel. End the evening with fresh seafood at a beachfront restaurant.
