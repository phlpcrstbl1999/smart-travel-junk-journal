Introduction

The Undiscovered Island of Quezon Province

Alabat Island is a quiet island municipality in Quezon province, situated in Quezon Bay between the Bondoc Peninsula and the Quezon mainland. Accessible only by ferry from Perez, Quezon, the island has remained largely off the tourist trail — a peaceful, unhurried destination of white sand beaches, mangrove forests, coconut plantations, and fishing communities that offers a genuine escape from the pace of Manila without the crowds of more famous Philippine island destinations.

The island covers about 150 square kilometers and is home to several municipalities, each with its own stretch of coastline, fishing villages, and local character. The beaches of Alabat are not the powdery white sand of Palawan or Boracay, but they are clean, quiet, and backed by coconut palms and mangrove forests — the kind of unspoiled coastal landscape that is increasingly rare near Manila. The surrounding waters are rich in marine life and the island's fishing communities still practice traditional fishing methods.

Alabat is best experienced as a slow travel destination — a place to rent a tricycle and explore the island's coastline at leisure, eat fresh seafood at the local carinderias, walk through the mangrove boardwalks, and experience the unhurried pace of provincial island life. It is not a destination for luxury resorts or organized tours — it is a destination for travelers who want to discover a piece of the Philippines that most visitors never see.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Alabat Island

Alabat Island is in Quezon province — about 180 km southeast of Manila. The journey takes about 4₱5 hours including the ferry crossing.

01

Bus from Manila to Perez, Quezon

Take a DLTB or JAC Liner bus from Buendia (Pasay) bound for Infanta or Real, Quezon and alight at Perez, Quezon — the jump-off point for the Alabat Island ferry. The bus ride takes about 3.5₱4 hours.

Manila to Perez: ~3.5₱4 hours  |  ~₱250–₱300
02

Ferry from Perez to Alabat

From the Perez port, take the passenger ferry to Alabat Island. The crossing takes about 20₱30 minutes. Ferries run several times daily — check the schedule at the port as it varies by season and weather conditions.

Ferry crossing: ~20₱30 min  |  ~₱50–₱80  |  Check schedule at port
03

Explore by Tricycle

On the island, hire a tricycle for the day to explore the coastline, beaches, and mangrove areas. Tricycle drivers double as guides and know the best spots. Negotiate a day rate for a full island tour — typically ₱500–₱800 for the whole day.

Tricycle day tour: ~₱500–₱800  |  Ask the driver for the best beaches
04

Accommodation

Alabat has basic guesthouses and homestays in the main town — accommodation is simple but clean and welcoming. For a more comfortable stay, some visitors base themselves in Perez or Lucena City on the mainland and do Alabat as a day trip. Book accommodation in advance as options are limited.

Guesthouses: ~₱500–₱1,000/night  |  Book in advance — limited options

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): The best time for Alabat — calm seas, clear skies, and the most pleasant conditions for beach walks and island exploration. The ferry crossing is also more reliable in calm weather.
  • Avoid typhoon season: Quezon province is in the typhoon belt — June to October can bring rough seas and heavy rain. Ferry services may be suspended during bad weather. Always check conditions before traveling.
  • Weekdays: Alabat sees very few tourists even on weekends — but weekdays are even quieter. The island is at its most peaceful and authentic on a weekday.
  • Pahiyas Festival (May 15): If visiting around mid-May, combine with the Pahiyas Festival in nearby Lucban — one of the most colorful harvest festivals in the Philippines.
📌

What to Do

  • Beach walks: The island's coastline has several quiet beaches backed by coconut palms — ideal for long, peaceful walks with no crowds. Ask your tricycle driver for the best stretches of sand.
  • Mangrove boardwalks: Alabat has well-preserved mangrove forests with boardwalks — a peaceful walk through the mangroves at low tide reveals the rich ecosystem of the island's coastal wetlands.
  • Fishing village visits: The island's fishing communities are welcoming and the traditional fishing methods still practiced here are fascinating to observe. Ask permission before photographing.
  • Fresh seafood: The local carinderias serve the freshest seafood in Quezon — grilled fish, shrimp, and crab caught that morning. Eat where the locals eat for the best and most affordable meals.
📌

Practical Tips

  • Bring cash: There are no ATMs on Alabat Island. Bring enough cash from Manila or Lucena City for your entire stay — accommodation, food, tricycle hire, and ferry fares.
  • Pack light: Accommodation is basic and storage is limited. Pack only what you need for a simple island stay — swimwear, light clothing, sunscreen, and insect repellent.
  • Insect repellent: Mangrove areas and coconut plantations can have mosquitoes, especially in the evening. Bring effective insect repellent.
  • Respect the community: Alabat is a quiet, conservative island community. Dress modestly when not on the beach and be respectful of local customs and privacy.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend a Weekend on Alabat Island

Two days on Alabat — beaches, mangroves, fresh seafood, and the slow pace of island life — is the perfect antidote to Manila's urban intensity.

Day 1
5:00 AM

Depart Manila

Take the early bus from Buendia to Perez, Quezon. The 3.5₱4 hour ride arrives in Perez by mid-morning. Head straight to the port for the ferry to Alabat.

10:00 AM

Ferry to Alabat & Check In

Take the 20₱30 minute ferry crossing to Alabat Island. Check in to your guesthouse or homestay, then hire a tricycle for the afternoon island exploration.

12:00 PM

Fresh Seafood Lunch

Have lunch at a local carinderia — grilled fish, shrimp, and rice. The freshest and most affordable seafood in Quezon province, served in a simple setting with a view of the bay.

2:00 PM

Coastline & Beach Walk

Explore the island's coastline by tricycle — stop at the quiet beaches, walk through the coconut plantations, and watch the fishing boats return in the late afternoon.

Day 2
6:00 AM

Sunrise & Mangrove Walk

Wake up for the sunrise over Quezon Bay — the morning light on the water is beautiful. Walk through the mangrove boardwalks at low tide before the heat of the day builds.

9:00 AM

Fishing Village Visit

Visit one of the island's fishing communities — watch the morning catch being sorted and sold, and talk to the fishermen about life on the island. A genuine window into provincial Philippine life.

12:00 PM

Ferry Back & Kamay ni Hesus

Take the afternoon ferry back to Perez. Drive to Lucban to visit Kamay ni Hesus shrine before the drive back to Manila — a perfect spiritual complement to the island escape.
