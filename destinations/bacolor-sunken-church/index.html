Introduction & History

Buried by Pinatubo, Still Standing

The San Guillermo Parish Church of Bacolor, Pampanga was founded in 1576 by Augustinian missionaries — making it one of the oldest churches in the Philippines. For over four centuries, it stood as the spiritual center of the Kapampangan community of Bacolor, surviving earthquakes, wars, and the passage of time.

Then, in 1991, Mount Pinatubo erupted in one of the largest volcanic eruptions of the 20th century. The eruption itself was devastating, but the lahar flows that followed — rivers of volcanic ash and debris that poured down the mountain's slopes for years afterward — proved equally destructive. Successive lahar flows buried the town of Bacolor under meters of volcanic material, and the San Guillermo Church was gradually submerged until only its upper walls, bell tower, and roof remained visible above the lahar surface.

Rather than demolish the buried structure, the community chose to preserve it as a living memorial to the Pinatubo disaster. The church continues to function as an active parish — masses are held inside the partially buried structure, with the congregation entering through what were once the upper windows. The result is one of the most extraordinary and moving heritage experiences in the Philippines: a church where you walk in at roofline level and look down into the buried nave below.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Bacolor Church

Bacolor Sunken Church is located in Bacolor, Pampanga — about 80 km north of Manila. It's easily accessible via the NLEX expressway.

01

Bus from Manila to San Fernando

Take a bus from Cubao or Pasay to San Fernando, Pampanga — the provincial capital. Genesis Transport and other bus lines serve this route. Travel time is about 1.5₱2 hours via NLEX.

Travel time: ~1.5₱2 hours  |  ~₱100–₱150 one-way
02

San Fernando to Bacolor

From San Fernando, take a jeepney or tricycle to Bacolor municipality — about 15₱20 minutes. The church is located in the Bacolor town center and is visible from the main road — the partially buried bell tower is unmistakable.

Jeepney to Bacolor: ~15₱20 min  |  ~₱20–₱40
03

Drive from Manila

Driving from Manila via NLEX takes about 1₱1.5 hours. Exit at the San Fernando interchange and follow signs to Bacolor. The church is well-signposted and easy to find. Parking is available near the church grounds.

Drive: ~1₱1.5 hours  |  Parking available near the church
04

Arrive at the Church

The church is located on the Bacolor town plaza. Entry to the grounds is free. The church is an active parish — masses are held regularly. The Pinatubo Museum nearby provides additional context about the 1991 eruption and its aftermath.

Free entry  |  Open daily  |  Pinatubo Museum nearby

Essential Visitor Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • Year-round: The church is open daily and accessible throughout the year. The lahar landscape is dramatic in any season.
  • Morning (7:00₱10:00 AM): The church is peaceful before tour groups arrive. Morning light on the partially buried facade is beautiful.
  • April 9 (Araw ng Kagitingan): Special masses and commemorative events are sometimes held at the church.
  • Avoid peak holiday weekends when the church can be crowded with pilgrims and tourists.
—

What to See

  • The exterior: Walk around the church to appreciate the full extent of the burial — the lahar surface is at roofline level, with only the upper walls and bell tower visible.
  • The interior: Enter through what were once the upper windows and look down into the buried nave. The experience of being at roofline level inside a functioning church is extraordinary.
  • The bell tower: The partially buried bell tower is the most iconic image of Bacolor — a symbol of resilience and survival.
  • The Pinatubo Museum: Located nearby, the museum provides essential context about the 1991 eruption and its impact on Pampanga.
📌

Etiquette & Respect

  • This is an active parish church. Dress modestly — cover shoulders and knees. Be quiet and respectful inside.
  • The church is a memorial to the Pinatubo disaster and the community's loss. Approach it with solemnity as well as curiosity.
  • Photography is allowed on the grounds and exterior. Be respectful when photographing inside during or near mass times.
  • Support the local community — buy from local vendors near the church and visit the Pinatubo Museum to learn more about the disaster's human impact.
Visitor Guide

How to Experience Bacolor Church

A complete guide to the church and a suggested Pampanga day tour.

At the Church
Step 1

Approach & Exterior

Approach the church from the town plaza. The sight of the partially buried structure — with the lahar surface at roofline level and only the upper walls and bell tower visible — is immediately striking. Walk around the exterior to appreciate the full extent of the burial.

Step 2

Enter the Church

Enter through the upper-level entrance — what were once the clerestory windows are now the main doors. Step inside and look down into the buried nave below. The experience of standing at roofline level inside a functioning church is unlike anything else in the Philippines.

Step 3

The Bell Tower

Walk to the partially buried bell tower — the most iconic image of Bacolor. The tower's lower sections are completely buried in lahar, with only the upper portion visible. It is a powerful symbol of the community's resilience in the face of natural disaster.

Step 4

Pinatubo Museum

Visit the nearby Pinatubo Museum for exhibits about the 1991 eruption — one of the largest volcanic eruptions of the 20th century. The museum provides essential context about the disaster's scale, its impact on Pampanga, and the ongoing recovery of affected communities.

Pampanga Day Tour
Morning

Bacolor Sunken Church

Start your Pampanga day at the sunken church — exterior walk, interior visit, bell tower, and Pinatubo Museum. Allow 1.5₱2 hours.

Midday

Nayong Pilipino Clark

Head to Clark Freeport Zone for Nayong Pilipino — a cultural theme park showcasing miniature replicas of Philippine heritage sites and regional culture. A perfect complement to the morning's heritage experience.

Afternoon

Explore San Fernando City — the Giant Lantern Festival capital of the Philippines. Visit the Pampanga Museum and enjoy Kapampangan cuisine — widely considered the best regional food in the Philippines.
