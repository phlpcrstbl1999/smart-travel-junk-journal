Introduction & Overview

World-Class Collections, Free for All

The National Museum of the Philippines is a complex of three major museum buildings facing Rizal Park in Manila — the National Museum of Fine Arts, the National Museum of Anthropology, and the National Museum of Natural History. All three are free to enter.

The Fine Arts building is home to Juan Luna's Spoliarium (1884) — a monumental 4₱7 meter painting that won the gold medal at the Madrid Exposition and is considered the greatest painting in Philippine history. The Natural History building features the stunning Tree of Life installation rising through its central atrium.

Together, the three museums house over 1.5 million artifacts — from the 4,000-year-old Balangay boats (the oldest watercraft found in Southeast Asia) to the complete skeleton of Lolong, the world's largest crocodile in captivity.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

Getting to the National Museum

The National Museum complex is on Padre Burgos Avenue, facing Rizal Park — easily reached by LRT or on foot from Intramuros.

01

LRT Line 1 — United Nations Station

Take LRT Line 1 to United Nations Station. Walk north along Taft Avenue and turn right on Padre Burgos Avenue — the museum complex is a 5-minute walk.

LRT Line 1: UN Station  |  ~5 min walk
02

From Rizal Park or Intramuros

The museum is directly across Padre Burgos Avenue from Rizal Park. Walk from the park in 2 minutes, or from Intramuros in about 15 minutes.

From Rizal Park: ~2 min  |  From Intramuros: ~15 min
03

Free Admission

All three museum buildings are free to enter. No tickets required — simply present a valid ID at the entrance. The museums are open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 AM₱5 PM.

Free admission  |  Tue–Sun: 10 AM₱5 PM
04

Plan Your Visit

Allow at least 3₱4 hours to visit all three buildings. Start with the Fine Arts building (Spoliarium), then the Natural History building (Tree of Life), then the Anthropology building (Balangay boats).

🚌 Recommended: 3₱4 hrs  |  Maps available at entrance

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Must-See: Fine Arts Building

  • Spoliarium (Juan Luna, 1884): The centerpiece of the collection — a monumental 4₱7 meter painting depicting dying gladiators in ancient Rome, widely interpreted as a metaphor for colonial oppression. The most important painting in Philippine history.
  • Parisian Life (Juan Luna): A smaller but equally celebrated Luna painting — a scene of Parisian café life with a Filipino woman at the center.
  • The Bulul collection: Sacred Ifugao rice deity figures — among the finest examples of Philippine indigenous sculpture.
  • The Rotunda: The building's magnificent domed rotunda is itself a work of art — look up.
📌

Must-See: Natural History Building

  • Tree of Life: A stunning sculptural installation rising through the building's central atrium — a 25-meter metal tree representing Philippine biodiversity.
  • Lolong the Crocodile: The preserved skeleton of Lolong — a 6.17-meter saltwater crocodile from Agusan del Sur, the largest crocodile in captivity when alive.
  • Balangay Boats: 4,000-year-old wooden boats discovered in Butuan — the oldest watercraft found in Southeast Asia and evidence of the Philippines' ancient maritime civilization.
  • Philippine flora and fauna: Comprehensive collections of endemic Philippine species — birds, mammals, marine life, and plants.
📌

Practical Tips

  • Free admission — bring a valid ID. No booking required for individual visitors.
  • Photography is permitted in most areas — no flash near paintings. The Tree of Life atrium is one of the most photogenic spaces in Manila.
  • Combine with Rizal Park: The museum is directly across from Rizal Park — visit both in a single day for the complete historic Manila experience.
  • Guided tours are available for groups — book in advance through the museum's official website.
Mini Itinerary

A Full Day of Manila Culture

Combine the National Museum with Intramuros, Rizal Park, and the Manila Baywalk sunset for the perfect Manila cultural day.

Morning
7:00 AM

Intramuros & Fort Santiago

Start the day at Intramuros — Fort Santiago, San Agustin Church, and a kalesa ride through the cobblestone streets. The walled city is most atmospheric in the early morning.

10:00 AM

National Museum of Fine Arts

Head to the National Museum of Fine Arts when it opens. Go straight to the Spoliarium — stand before the 4₱7 meter masterpiece and take your time. Then explore the rest of the collection.

Afternoon
1:00 PM

National Museum of Natural History

Cross to the Natural History building — the Tree of Life atrium, Lolong the crocodile, and the Balangay boats. Allow 1.5₱2 hours.

3:30 PM

Rizal Park & Manila Bay Sunset

Walk to Rizal Park for the afternoon. Stay for the legendary Manila Bay sunset from the park's western promenade — one of the most celebrated sunsets in Asia.
