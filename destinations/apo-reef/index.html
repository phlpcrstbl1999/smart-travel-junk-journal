Introduction

The Philippines' Greatest Marine Treasure

Apo Reef Natural Park is the crown jewel of Philippine marine conservation — the largest atoll-like coral reef system in the Philippines and the second largest contiguous coral reef in the world after Australia's Great Barrier Reef. Located in the Sulu Sea off the coast of Occidental Mindoro, the reef covers approximately 34 square kilometers and is surrounded by a protected marine park of over 270 square kilometers.

The reef's biodiversity is staggering — over 500 species of fish, 400 species of coral, sea turtles, sharks, manta rays, dolphins, and dugongs inhabit the reef and its surrounding waters. The visibility in the Sulu Sea is exceptional — often exceeding 30 meters — making Apo Reef one of the finest diving destinations in Southeast Asia. The reef's remote location has helped protect it from the overfishing and coastal development that have degraded many Philippine reefs.

Apo Reef is accessible from Sablayan, Occidental Mindoro — a 2₱3 hour boat ride from the mainland. The reef has a ranger station and basic facilities on Apo Island (not to be confused with Apo Island in Negros Oriental). Liveaboard dive trips from Puerto Galera or Coron are also popular ways to experience the reef. The best diving is on the reef's outer walls — dramatic drop-offs that plunge hundreds of meters into the Sulu Sea.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Apo Reef

Apo Reef is accessed from Sablayan, Occidental Mindoro — reached by ferry from Batangas City or by flying to San Jose, Mindoro.

01

Ferry from Batangas to Sablayan

Take a RORO ferry from Batangas City Port to Sablayan, Occidental Mindoro. The crossing takes about 8₱10 hours (overnight ferry recommended). Ferries operate several times weekly — check schedules with 2GO Travel or Montenegro Lines.

Batangas to Sablayan: ~8₱10 hours  |  ~₱500–₱800  |  Overnight ferry recommended
02

Fly to San Jose, Mindoro

Fly from Manila to San Jose, Occidental Mindoro (about 1 hour). From San Jose, take a bus or van to Sablayan (about 2 hours). This is the fastest option for reaching the reef.

Manila to San Jose: ~1 hour  |  San Jose to Sablayan: ~2 hours
03

Boat from Sablayan to Apo Reef

From Sablayan, hire a banca boat to Apo Reef — the crossing takes 2₱3 hours depending on sea conditions. Book through the Sablayan Tourism Office or a local dive operator. A ranger fee and park entrance fee are required.

🚌 Sablayan to Apo Reef: ~2₱3 hours  |  Park fee: ~₱200  |  Dive fee extra
04

Liveaboard Option

For serious divers, a liveaboard dive trip from Puerto Galera or Coron is the best way to experience Apo Reef — multiple dives per day over 2₱3 nights, covering all the major dive sites. Several liveaboard operators run regular trips to the reef.

Liveaboard: ~₱8,000₱15,000 per person  |  Best for serious divers

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Dive

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): The best time for Apo Reef — calm seas, excellent visibility (often 30m+), and the most reliable boat crossings from Sablayan. MarchMay is peak diving season.
  • Avoid JuneOctober: The southwest monsoon (habagat) brings rough seas and poor visibility. The boat crossing from Sablayan can be dangerous and is often cancelled. Most dive operators suspend trips during this period.
  • Full moon: Diving around the full moon offers the chance to see spawning aggregations of fish — one of the most spectacular natural events on the reef.
  • Early morning dives: The first dive of the day, before the sun is high, offers the best light for photography and the most active marine life.
🤼

Diving Apo Reef

  • Experience required: Apo Reef is not a beginner dive destination. The outer wall dives involve strong currents and significant depths. An Advanced Open Water certification and experience with drift diving is recommended.
  • Top dive sites: Apo Reef Wall (dramatic drop-off with sharks and large pelagics), Shark Ridge (grey reef sharks and whitetip sharks), Binangaan Pass (strong current, large schools of fish), and the Lighthouse area (coral gardens, turtles).
  • Marine megafauna: Apo Reef is one of the best places in the Philippines to see whale sharks, thresher sharks, hammerhead sharks, manta rays, and dolphins. Sightings are not guaranteed but are common.
  • No touching the reef: Apo Reef is a strictly protected marine park. Do not touch, stand on, or collect anything from the reef. Violations carry heavy fines.
📌

Staying at the Reef

  • Apo Island ranger station: Basic accommodation is available at the ranger station on Apo Island — simple dormitory-style rooms and camping. Bring your own food and water as supplies are very limited.
  • Liveaboard recommended: For the best experience, a liveaboard dive boat is the most comfortable and efficient way to dive Apo Reef — you sleep on the boat and dive multiple times per day.
  • Sablayan as base: Sablayan has basic guesthouses and a few dive operators who run day trips to the reef. A good base for budget travelers.
  • Bring everything: There are no shops or restaurants at the reef. Bring all food, water, sunscreen, and dive equipment from Sablayan or your liveaboard.
Mini Itinerary

How to Plan a 3-Day Apo Reef Dive Trip

Three days at Apo Reef — arriving by liveaboard or banca from Sablayan — gives you time to dive all the major sites and experience the full biodiversity of the reef.

Day 1
Day 0 — Evening

Depart Manila — Overnight Ferry

Take the overnight ferry from Batangas City to Sablayan. Arrive in Sablayan in the morning, meet your dive operator, and prepare for the 2₱3 hour boat crossing to the reef.

Afternoon

First Dives — Coral Gardens

Arrive at the reef and begin with the shallower coral garden sites near the lighthouse — a gentle introduction to the reef's extraordinary biodiversity. Turtles, reef fish, and pristine coral formations.

Days 2₱3
Morning

Apo Reef Wall & Shark Ridge

The signature dives — the outer wall drop-off with grey reef sharks, whitetip sharks, and large pelagics. The wall plunges hundreds of meters into the Sulu Sea. Visibility often exceeds 30 meters.

Afternoon

Binangaan Pass — Drift Dive

The most exhilarating dive on the reef — a drift dive through the pass with strong currents and enormous schools of fish. Barracuda, jacks, and snappers in the thousands.

Evening

Night Dive & Return

A night dive on the reef reveals a completely different ecosystem — octopus, cuttlefish, sleeping turtles, and bioluminescent plankton. Return to Sablayan on Day 3 for the journey back to Manila.
