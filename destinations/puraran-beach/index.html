Introduction & Overview

The Island of the Howling Wind

Puraran Beach is located in Baras, Catanduanes — an island province off the eastern coast of Bicol known as the "Land of the Howling Winds." The beach is famous for its powerful surf break called "The Majestic," a barreling right-hand reef break that consistently produces world-class waves, especially from August to November.

Beyond surfing, Puraran captivates with its raw, unspoiled beauty — a long stretch of grey-black sand, crystal-clear water, and a backdrop of lush green hills. The remoteness of the location means the beach remains uncrowded and pristine, offering a true off-the-beaten-path experience.

Puraran has hosted international surfing competitions and is considered one of the top surf destinations in Asia. Even for non-surfers, watching the Majestic break from the shore is a breathtaking spectacle.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Puraran Beach

Puraran Beach is in Baras, Catanduanes — about 45 km from Virac, the provincial capital. Getting there requires a flight or ferry to Catanduanes, then a land trip to Baras.

01

Fly to Virac, Catanduanes

Fly from Manila to Virac Airport in Catanduanes. Philippine Airlines and Cebu Pacific operate this route. The flight takes about 1 hour. This is the fastest way to reach the island.

Flight: ~1 hr  |  ~₱1,500₱3,500 one-way
02

Alternatively: Ferry from Tabaco, Albay

Take a bus from Manila to Tabaco City, Albay, then board a ferry to San Andres Port, Catanduanes. The ferry takes about 2.5₱3 hours. From San Andres, take a van or bus to Virac (~1 hour).

Ferry: ~2.5₱3 hrs  |  ~₱200–₱350
03

Van or Bus to Baras

From Virac, take a van or bus bound for Baras (~1.5₱2 hours). The road passes through scenic mountain terrain. From Baras town proper, hire a tricycle or habal-habal to Puraran Beach (~20 minutes).

Van: ~₱80–₱120  |  Tricycle: ~₱100–₱150
04

Accommodation at Puraran

Several small surf camps and guesthouses operate directly at Puraran Beach. Book in advance during peak surf season (AugustNovember). Most accommodations offer surfboard rentals and lessons for beginners.

Surf Season: AugNov  |  Book ahead in peak season

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • August — November (Surf Season): The best waves arrive with the northeast monsoon. The Majestic is at its most powerful and consistent during these months.
  • March — May (Calm Season): Smaller waves, ideal for beginners and snorkeling. The water is clearer and calmer.
  • Typhoon awareness: Catanduanes is one of the most typhoon-prone provinces in the Philippines. Always check weather forecasts before traveling.
  • Weekdays are quieter: Even during peak season, Puraran remains relatively uncrowded compared to other surf destinations.
🏄

Surfing Tips

  • The Majestic is for experienced surfers: The reef break produces powerful, hollow waves. Beginners should stick to the beach break or take lessons first.
  • Reef shoes and rash guards are essential — the reef is sharp and the sun is intense.
  • Local surf instructors are available at the beach camps. A lesson costs around ₱500–₱800 including board rental.
  • Respect local surfers and follow surf etiquette — the lineup can get competitive during peak swell.
🎒

What to Pack & Know

  • Cash only — there are no ATMs near Puraran. Bring enough cash from Virac for your entire stay.
  • Basic supplies — the area is remote. Stock up on snacks, medicine, and toiletries in Virac before heading to Baras.
  • Try Catanduanes specialties: Laing (taro in coconut milk), kinunot (shark in coconut milk), and fresh seafood are the local highlights.
  • Combine with Binurong Point — the dramatic cliff viewpoint is about 30 minutes from Puraran and is one of the most stunning views in Catanduanes.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend 2 Days in Catanduanes

Combine Puraran Beach with Binurong Point and the natural wonders of eastern Catanduanes for an unforgettable island adventure.

Day 1
Morning

Arrive & Settle at Puraran

Arrive at Puraran Beach and check into your surf camp. Take a morning walk along the beach, watch the surfers tackle the Majestic, and get your bearings. The grey-black sand and powerful waves create an atmosphere unlike any other beach in the Philippines.

Afternoon

Surf Lesson or Swim

Take a beginner surf lesson with a local instructor on the beach break, or simply swim and snorkel in the calmer sections of the cove. The water is crystal clear and the marine life around the reef is rich and colorful.

Sunset

Sunset at the Beach

Watch the sunset from the beach as the surfers catch their last waves of the day. The sky turns brilliant shades of orange and pink over the Pacific — a perfect end to your first day on the island.

Day 2
Morning

Binurong Point

Take a habal-habal to Binurong Point — a dramatic cliff viewpoint overlooking the Pacific Ocean and the rugged Catanduanes coastline. The panoramic view from the top is one of the most breathtaking in the Philippines. Allow 2₱3 hours for the visit.

Afternoon

More Surfing or Explore Baras

Return to Puraran for more surfing, or explore Baras town for local food and culture. Try kinunot — the Catanduanes specialty of shark meat cooked in coconut milk and malunggay — at a local eatery.

Evening

Return to Virac

Head back to Virac for your onward journey. If time permits, visit the Bato Church — a centuries-old Spanish colonial church — on your way back through the island's interior.
