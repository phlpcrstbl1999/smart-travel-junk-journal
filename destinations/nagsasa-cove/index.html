Introduction

Zambales' Best-Kept Secret

Located in San Antonio, Zambales, Nagsasa Cove is often described as the quieter, more spacious alternative to the famous Anawangin Cove. Like Anawangin, Nagsasa features grey volcanic sand and a forest of Agoho pine trees — but the cove is wider, the beach is longer, and the number of visitors is significantly lower, giving it a more secluded and pristine character.

What sets Nagsasa apart from Anawangin is its freshwater lagoon — a calm, clear body of water hidden behind the beach, separated from the sea by a narrow strip of sand. The lagoon is perfect for swimming, kayaking, and simply floating in the cool, still water surrounded by pine trees. The contrast between the open sea on one side and the peaceful lagoon on the other makes Nagsasa one of the most varied and rewarding coves in Zambales.

Nagsasa is accessible only by boat from Pundaquit Beach — the same jump-off point as Anawangin. Most visitors combine both coves in a single boat trip, spending time at each before continuing to Capones Island. For those who want a more secluded camping experience than Anawangin, Nagsasa is the better choice — fewer visitors, more space, and the added bonus of the freshwater lagoon.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Nagsasa Cove

Nagsasa Cove is located in San Antonio, Zambales — about 160 km northwest of Manila. The jump-off point is Pundaquit Beach, the same as for Anawangin Cove.

01

Bus from Manila to San Antonio

Victory Liner operates buses from Cubao or Pasay to San Antonio, Zambales. Travel time is about 3₱4 hours. Alternatively, take a bus to Olongapo City and then a jeepney to San Antonio.

Travel time: ~3₱4 hours  |  ~₱200–₱300 one-way
02

San Antonio to Pundaquit Beach

From San Antonio town center, take a tricycle to Pundaquit Beach. Register at the barangay hall and pay the environmental fee. Negotiate your boat route with the boatman — include Nagsasa, Anawangin, and Capones in one trip.

Tricycle to Pundaquit: ~₱50–₱80  |  Environmental fee: ~₱50–₱100
03

Boat from Pundaquit to Nagsasa

Nagsasa Cove is about 30₱40 minutes by boat from Pundaquit — slightly farther than Anawangin. The boat ride passes along the dramatic Zambales coastline with views of the pine-covered hills and the open South China Sea.

🚌 Boat to Nagsasa: ~₱1,200₱1,800  |  — ~30₱40 minutes
04

Combine with Anawangin & Capones

Most visitors combine Nagsasa with Anawangin Cove and Capones Island Lighthouse in a single boat trip. Negotiate a full-day island hopping package with your boatman at Pundaquit for the best value.

Full island hopping: ~₱2,000₱3,000  |  Covers all 3 destinations

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): Best time — calm seas, clear skies, and the most comfortable camping and swimming conditions.
  • Overnight camping: Like Anawangin, Nagsasa is best experienced overnight — the cove is even more magical without the day-trippers.
  • Weekdays: Nagsasa already has fewer visitors than Anawangin — on weekdays it can feel completely private.
  • Avoid typhoon season (June — October): Rough seas make the boat crossing dangerous.
📌

The Freshwater Lagoon

  • Swim in the lagoon — the calm, clear freshwater is perfect for swimming and floating. A refreshing contrast to the saltwater sea.
  • Kayak the lagoon — kayak rentals are sometimes available at the cove. Paddling through the lagoon surrounded by pine trees is extraordinary.
  • The lagoon is shallow in some sections — wade carefully and watch for submerged rocks.
  • Don't use soap or shampoo in the lagoon — it is a natural ecosystem. Respect the water quality.
—

Camping & Practical Tips

  • Bring all supplies — food, water, cooking equipment, and camping gear. No stores inside the cove.
  • Leave no trace. Pack out all your trash — the cove's pristine condition depends on every visitor's responsibility.
  • Nagsasa has more space than Anawangin — you can find a more private camping spot away from other groups.
  • The lagoon provides freshwater for washing — but bring your own drinking water.
Visitor Guide

How to Experience Nagsasa Cove

A complete guide to Nagsasa and the full Zambales cove island hopping experience.

At Nagsasa
Step 1

Arrive & Explore the Beach

Arrive at Nagsasa and walk the full length of the beach. The cove is wider than Anawangin — the grey sand stretches further and the pine forest is denser. Find your camping spot and set up before exploring further.

Step 2

Discover the Freshwater Lagoon

Walk behind the beach to find the freshwater lagoon. The transition from the open sea to the calm, enclosed lagoon is dramatic. Swim, float, or simply sit by the water and enjoy the extraordinary setting.

Step 3

Swimming & Snorkeling

Swim in the sea and snorkel around the rocky formations at the edges of the cove. The water is clear and the marine life around the rocks is diverse. Bring your own snorkeling gear for the best experience.

Step 4

Sunset & Camping

Watch the sunset from the beach — the South China Sea sunsets from Nagsasa are as spectacular as those from Anawangin. Cook dinner, enjoy the campfire, and sleep under the stars in one of the most peaceful settings near Manila.

Zambales Cove Hop
Morning

Anawangin Cove

Start at the iconic Anawangin — grey sand, pine trees, and the most photographed cove in Zambales. Swim, explore, and enjoy the morning atmosphere.

Midday

Nagsasa Cove

Continue to Nagsasa for the freshwater lagoon, wider beach, and fewer crowds. Have lunch here and explore the lagoon before continuing.

Afternoon

Capones Island Lighthouse

End your Zambales cove hop at Capones Island — hike to the 19th-century Spanish lighthouse for panoramic views of the coastline before the boat back to Pundaquit.
