Introduction & History

Where the Philippines Was Born

Intramuros — Latin for "within the walls" — is the original city of Manila, founded by Spanish conquistador Miguel Lpez de Legazpi in 1571. Enclosed by massive stone walls and a moat, it served as the seat of Spanish colonial power in Asia for over 300 years.

Within its 64 hectares stand Fort Santiago, the Manila Cathedral, San Agustin Church (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), the Casa Manila Museum, and a network of cobblestone streets that have survived earthquakes, revolutions, and the devastation of World War II.

Today, Intramuros is the most historically significant district in the Philippines · a living museum where horse-drawn kalesa still clatter over the same stones walked by Rizal, Magellan's successors, and generations of Filipinos who shaped the nation.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

Getting to Intramuros

Intramuros is in the heart of Manila — easily reached by LRT, jeepney, taxi, or on foot from Rizal Park.

01

LRT Line 1 — Central Station

Take LRT Line 1 to Central Station (formerly UN Avenue). Intramuros is a short walk north across Padre Burgos Avenue. The LRT is the fastest way to reach the area from most parts of Manila.

LRT Line 1: Central Station  |  ~10 min walk
02

From Rizal Park

Intramuros is directly adjacent to Rizal Park (Luneta). Walk north from the park's main area to reach the Intramuros walls and the main entrance gates in about 10 minutes.

Walk from Luneta: ~10 min  |  Combine both in one visit
03

Kalesa (Horse-Drawn Carriage)

Hire a kalesa from outside the walls for a guided tour of Intramuros by horse-drawn carriage — the most atmospheric way to explore the cobblestone streets and colonial architecture.

Kalesa tour: ~₱300–₱600  |  — ~30₱45 min
04

Bamboo Bike Tour

Join a bamboo bicycle tour of Intramuros — a popular guided experience that covers all the major sites within the walls at a leisurely pace, with a knowledgeable guide providing historical context.

Bike tour: ~₱500–₱800  |  Guide included

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • Early morning (7₱10 AM): The best time to explore — cooler temperatures, softer light for photography, and fewer crowds before the tour groups arrive.
  • December — February: The coolest and most comfortable months for walking the cobblestone streets.
  • Avoid midday heat: Manila's heat is intense from 11 AM₱3 PM. Plan your visit around the cooler morning or late afternoon hours.
  • June 12 (Independence Day): Special events and ceremonies are held in Intramuros — a meaningful time to visit.
📌

Must-See Sites

  • Fort Santiago: The 16th-century citadel where Jos Rizal was imprisoned. Walk the Rizal Shrine and see the footprints marking his final walk to Bagumbayan.
  • San Agustin Church: The oldest stone church in the Philippines (1607) and a UNESCO World Heritage Site — the only building in Intramuros to survive WWII intact.
  • Manila Cathedral: The seat of the Archdiocese of Manila, rebuilt eight times since 1571. The current structure dates from 1958.
  • Casa Manila Museum: A beautifully reconstructed 19th-century colonial house — the best window into ilustrado life in Spanish Manila.
📌

Practical Tips

  • Wear comfortable walking shoes — the cobblestone streets are uneven and can be slippery.
  • Hire a licensed guide for Fort Santiago and San Agustin Church — the historical context they provide transforms the experience.
  • Combine with Rizal Park: Intramuros and Rizal Park are adjacent — visit both in a single morning for the complete historic Manila experience.
  • Photography: The golden hour light on the stone walls and churches is extraordinary — arrive early or return at sunset.
Mini Itinerary

A Morning in Intramuros

Combine Intramuros with Fort Santiago, Rizal Park, and the National Museum for the complete historic Manila experience.

Morning
7:00 AM

Fort Santiago

Start at Fort Santiago before the crowds arrive. Walk the Rizal Shrine, see the cell where the national hero was imprisoned, and follow the bronze footprints to the execution site at Bagumbayan.

9:00 AM

San Agustin Church & Museum

Visit San Agustin Church — the oldest stone church in the Philippines and the only Intramuros building to survive WWII. The adjacent museum houses one of the finest collections of religious art in Asia.

10:30 AM

Kalesa Ride & Manila Cathedral

Take a kalesa ride through the cobblestone streets to the Manila Cathedral. The horse-drawn carriage is the most atmospheric way to experience the colonial streetscape.

Afternoon
12:00 PM

Lunch at Barbara's Heritage Restaurant

Have lunch at Barbara's Heritage Restaurant inside Intramuros — Filipino cuisine in a beautifully restored colonial setting, with cultural shows during lunch service.

2:00 PM

Rizal Park

Walk to Rizal Park (Luneta) — the national park where Jos Rizal was executed on December 30, 1896. Visit the Rizal Monument, the Chinese and Japanese Gardens, and the open-air concert area.

4:00 PM

National Museum of the Philippines

Spend the late afternoon at the National Museum — free admission, world-class collections, and the iconic Juan Luna painting Spoliarium. One of the finest museums in Southeast Asia.
