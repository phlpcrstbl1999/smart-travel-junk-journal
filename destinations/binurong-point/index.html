Introduction & Overview

The Edge of the Island

Binurong Point is a dramatic headland in Baras, Catanduanes, where towering limestone cliffs plunge directly into the Pacific Ocean. Standing at the edge, you look out over an unobstructed expanse of deep blue water stretching to the horizon — with no land between you and the open Pacific.

The viewpoint is reached via a short trek through coconut groves and rolling hills. The path opens suddenly to reveal the cliffs and the ocean below — a moment that consistently leaves visitors speechless. The combination of the rugged coastline, the crashing waves far below, and the sheer scale of the Pacific makes Binurong Point one of the most dramatic natural viewpoints in the Philippines.

The area around Binurong Point is also home to natural rock formations, sea caves, and tidal pools that can be explored at low tide. It is best combined with a visit to nearby Puraran Beach for a full day in eastern Catanduanes.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Binurong Point

Binurong Point is in Baras, Catanduanes, near Puraran Beach. The journey requires reaching Catanduanes island first, then traveling to the eastern side of the island.

01

Fly or Ferry to Catanduanes

Fly from Manila to Virac Airport (~1 hour), or take a bus to Tabaco City, Albay and board a ferry to San Andres, Catanduanes (~2.5₱3 hours). Virac is the provincial capital and main hub of the island.

Flight: ~1 hr  |  Ferry: ~2.5₱3 hrs
02

Van or Bus to Baras

From Virac, take a van or bus to Baras (~1.5₱2 hours). The road winds through the mountainous interior of the island with scenic views along the way. Baras is the jump-off point for both Binurong Point and Puraran Beach.

Van: ~₱80–₱120  |  — ~1.5₱2 hrs
03

Habal-Habal to Binurong Point

From Baras town proper, hire a habal-habal (motorcycle taxi) to Binurong Point (~20₱30 minutes). The driver will wait for you while you explore. Alternatively, rent a motorcycle in Virac for more flexibility.

Habal-habal: ~₱150–₱250 round trip
04

Trek to the Viewpoint

From the drop-off point, a short 15₱20 minute trek through coconut groves leads to the cliff edge. The path is relatively easy but can be slippery after rain. Wear sturdy footwear and bring water. A local guide is recommended.

Trek: ~15₱20 min  |  Guide fee: ~₱100–₱200

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • March — June (Dry Season): Best visibility and safest conditions for the cliff trek. Clear skies offer the most dramatic ocean views.
  • August — November: Coincides with surf season at Puraran — combine both in one trip. Expect some rain but the dramatic skies add to the atmosphere.
  • Avoid typhoon season peaks: Catanduanes is the most typhoon-prone province in the Philippines. Check PAGASA forecasts before traveling.
  • Early morning visits offer the clearest views before afternoon clouds build up over the Pacific.
🧦

Trekking Tips

  • Wear closed-toe shoes — the path can be muddy and the cliff edges are uneven. Flip-flops are not suitable.
  • Stay back from the edge — the cliffs are steep and there are no safety railings. Exercise extreme caution, especially in wet conditions.
  • Hire a local guide from Baras — they know the safest paths and can point out the best viewpoints and rock formations below.
  • Bring water and snacks — there are no vendors at the viewpoint. Stock up in Baras before heading out.
📷

Photography & Packing

  • Wide-angle lens captures the full scale of the cliffs and the Pacific horizon in a single frame.
  • Golden hour (6₱8 AM): The morning light illuminates the cliffs and the ocean turns deep blue — the best conditions for photography.
  • Combine with Puraran Beach — both are in Baras and can be visited in a single day trip from Virac.
  • Cash only — bring enough from Virac. There are no ATMs in Baras.
Mini Itinerary

A Day in Eastern Catanduanes

Combine Binurong Point with Puraran Beach for a full day exploring the wild, dramatic eastern coast of Catanduanes island.

Morning
6:00 AM

Depart Virac for Baras

Take an early van from Virac to Baras. The 1.5₱2 hour journey through the island's mountainous interior is scenic in itself — rolling hills, coconut groves, and glimpses of the Pacific as you approach the eastern coast.

8:00 AM

Binurong Point at Sunrise

Arrive at Binurong Point in the morning for the clearest views. Trek to the cliff edge and take in the panoramic spectacle of the Pacific Ocean stretching to the horizon. The morning light is perfect for photography.

10:00 AM

Explore the Rock Formations

With a local guide, explore the rock formations and tidal pools at the base of the cliffs (accessible at low tide). Sea caves, natural arches, and colorful marine life make this a rewarding extension of the cliff visit.

Afternoon
12:00 PM

Lunch in Baras

Return to Baras town for lunch at a local eatery. Try kinunot (shark in coconut milk) or fresh grilled fish — the seafood in Catanduanes is exceptionally fresh and affordable.

1:30 PM

Puraran Beach

Head to Puraran Beach for the afternoon. Watch the surfers tackle the Majestic break, swim in the cove, or take a beginner surf lesson. The beach's raw, unspoiled beauty is the perfect complement to the dramatic cliffs of Binurong Point.

5:00 PM

Return to Virac

Catch the late afternoon van back to Virac. If you're staying overnight in Catanduanes, consider spending the night at one of the surf camps at Puraran for the full island experience.
