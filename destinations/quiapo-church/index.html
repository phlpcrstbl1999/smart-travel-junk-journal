Introduction & History

The Most Venerated Image in the Philippines

The Minor Basilica of the Black Nazarene — known simply as Quiapo Church — was founded in 1586 by the Franciscan order. It is the home of the Black Nazarene: a life-size dark-skinned image of Jesus Christ carrying the cross, carved from mesquite wood in Mexico and brought to Manila in 1606.

The Black Nazarene is the most venerated religious image in the Philippines. Millions of Filipino Catholics believe it has miraculous powers — healing the sick, granting prayers, and protecting devotees. The image is the center of the annual Traslacion procession on January 9, when an estimated 6₱9 million barefoot devotees crowd the streets of Manila in one of the largest religious gatherings in the world.

The church and its surrounding plaza are the spiritual and commercial heart of Quiapo district — surrounded by the famous Quiapo market, herbal medicine vendors, and the vibrant street life of one of Manila's oldest neighborhoods.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

Getting to Quiapo Church

Quiapo Church is in the heart of Manila — easily reached by LRT or jeepney from most parts of the city.

01

LRT Line 1 — Carriedo Station

Take LRT Line 1 to Carriedo Station. Walk south along Quezon Boulevard for about 5 minutes to reach Quiapo Church and Plaza Miranda.

LRT Line 1: Carriedo Station  |  ~5 min walk
02

From Binondo

Quiapo is a short walk from Binondo — cross the Quezon Bridge over the Pasig River and walk south along Quezon Boulevard (~10 minutes). Combine both neighborhoods in a single morning.

Walk from Binondo: ~10 min  |  Via Quezon Bridge
03

Friday Mass & Devotion

Fridays are the most important day at Quiapo Church — thousands of devotees come to touch the image of the Black Nazarene and offer prayers. The atmosphere is intensely devotional and deeply moving.

Friday devotion: most atmospheric  |  — Masses throughout the day
04

Quiapo Market

The streets around the church are lined with the famous Quiapo market — herbal medicine vendors, religious goods, fabrics, electronics, and street food. The market is one of the most vibrant in Manila.

Market: surrounding streets  |  Herbal medicine: Hidalgo Street

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

The Black Nazarene

  • January 9 — Traslacion: The annual procession of the Black Nazarene — 6₱9 million barefoot devotees crowd the streets of Manila. One of the largest religious gatherings in the world. Extraordinary to witness, but extremely crowded.
  • Good Friday: Another major day of devotion — the church and surrounding streets are packed with pilgrims.
  • Friday devotion: Every Friday, thousands of devotees come to touch the image and offer prayers. The most accessible time to experience the devotion.
  • The image: The Black Nazarene is kept in a glass case inside the church — visible to all visitors.
📌

Quiapo Market

  • Herbal medicine (Hidalgo Street): The famous herbal medicine vendors of Quiapo — traditional Filipino and Chinese medicinal plants, roots, and preparations.
  • Religious goods: The streets around the church are lined with vendors selling religious images, rosaries, candles, and devotional items.
  • Fabrics and textiles: Quiapo is Manila's fabric district — dozens of shops selling cloth, lace, and sewing materials at wholesale prices.
  • Street food: Kwek-kwek (battered quail eggs), fish balls, and other Manila street food classics are sold around the plaza.
📌

Practical Tips

  • Dress modestly when entering the church — shoulders and knees should be covered.
  • Be aware of pickpockets in the crowded market area — keep valuables secure.
  • Combine with Binondo: Quiapo and Binondo are a short walk apart — visit both in a single morning for the complete historic Manila experience.
  • Photography: Photography is permitted inside the church but be respectful of worshippers.
Mini Itinerary

Old Manila in a Morning

Combine Quiapo Church with Binondo, Intramuros, and the National Museum for the complete historic Manila experience.

Morning
7:00 AM

Binondo Breakfast

Start in Binondo for breakfast — goto, dim sum, and hopia from the world's oldest Chinatown. Then walk across Quezon Bridge to Quiapo.

9:00 AM

Quiapo Church & Market

Visit Quiapo Church — see the Black Nazarene, attend a morning mass, and explore the surrounding market. Browse the herbal medicine vendors on Hidalgo Street.

11:00 AM

Intramuros

Take a jeepney or walk to Intramuros for the rest of the morning — Fort Santiago, San Agustin Church, and Manila Cathedral.

Afternoon
2:00 PM

National Museum

Spend the afternoon at the National Museum of the Philippines — free admission, the Spoliarium, and the Tree of Life installation.

5:30 PM

Manila Bay Sunset

End the day at Rizal Park for the legendary Manila Bay sunset — the perfect finale to a day of historic Manila.
