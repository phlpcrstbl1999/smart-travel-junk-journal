Introduction & History

The Ancestral Home of President Quirino

Built in the 1830s by the wealthy Syquia family, the Syquia Mansion is one of the finest examples of Ilocano-Chinese Mestizo architecture in the Philippines. The two-story structure features the characteristic design of Vigan's ancestral houses — a stone ground floor with a wooden upper story, wide capiz shell windows, and a red-tiled roof.

The mansion became historically significant as the birthplace and childhood home of Elpidio Quirino, who served as the sixth President of the Philippines from 1948 to 1953. Quirino was born in Vigan in 1890 and grew up in this house before rising to become one of the most important political figures in post-war Philippine history. His presidency was marked by efforts to rebuild the country after the devastation of World War II.

In 1978, the mansion was converted into a public museum by the National Historical Commission of the Philippines. Today, visitors can explore the well-preserved interiors — original furniture, personal belongings, family photographs, and historical documents that paint a vivid picture of 19th-century Ilocano life and the Quirino family's remarkable journey from Vigan to Malacaang Palace.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Syquia Mansion

The Syquia Mansion is located at Quirino Boulevard, Vigan City, Ilocos Sur — right in the heart of the UNESCO heritage zone, just steps from Calle Crisologo.

01

Take an Overnight Bus from Manila

Partas, Dominion, and Florida Bus Lines operate overnight buses from Cubao or Pasay directly to Vigan. Buses depart between 9₱11 PM and arrive in Vigan by 6₱7 AM. Book a deluxe or premium seat for comfort.

Travel time: ~8₱9 hours  |  ~₱700–₱1,100 one-way
02

Fly to Laoag then Bus to Vigan

Fly from Manila to Laoag International Airport, then take a bus or van south to Vigan — about 1.5₱2 hours. This is faster overall if you're also visiting Ilocos Norte attractions like Paoay Church and the Sand Dunes.

Flight: ~1 hour  |  Bus to Vigan: ~1.5₱2 hours
03

Walk from Calle Crisologo

The Syquia Mansion is located on Quirino Boulevard, just a short walk from Calle Crisologo and Plaza Salcedo. From the heritage zone, it's a 3₱5 minute walk. Look for the blue National Historical Commission marker at the entrance.

Walking from Calle Crisologo: ~3₱5 minutes  |  Quirino Blvd, Vigan
04

Combine with a Vigan Heritage Tour

The Syquia Mansion is best visited as part of a full Vigan heritage day. Combine it with Calle Crisologo, Saint Paul Cathedral, Plaza Salcedo, and a kalesa ride through the heritage zone for a complete Vigan experience.

Entry fee: ~₱30–₱50  |  Open TueSun 9:00 AM — 5:00 PM

Essential Visitor Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • Weekday Mornings (9:00₱11:00 AM): Fewer tourists, cooler temperatures, and better natural light through the capiz shell windows. The mansion is most peaceful on weekday mornings.
  • November — April (Dry Season): Best weather for exploring Vigan. Clear skies and comfortable temperatures make walking the heritage zone enjoyable.
  • Avoid Mondays: The museum is closed on Mondays. Plan your visit for Tuesday through Sunday.
  • January (Vigan Festival of the Arts): The city comes alive with cultural events. The mansion may have special exhibits and extended hours during the festival.
📌

What to See Inside

  • Ground Floor: The stone-walled ground floor originally served as a storage area and stable. Now houses introductory exhibits about the Syquia family and Vigan's history.
  • Upper Floor — Living Quarters: The main living area with original 19th-century furniture, capiz shell windows, and period decor. The wooden floors creak with history.
  • President Quirino's Room: The bedroom where Elpidio Quirino was born and grew up. Personal belongings, childhood photographs, and family memorabilia.
  • Presidential Artifacts: Documents, awards, official photographs, and memorabilia from Quirino's presidency (1948₱1953).
  • The Azotea: The open terrace at the back of the house — a classic feature of Ilocano ancestral homes. Offers a quiet view of the heritage zone.
📷

Photography & Etiquette

  • Photography is allowed throughout the mansion. The capiz shell windows create beautiful diffused light — perfect for interior shots.
  • No flash photography near fragile artifacts and documents. Use natural light or a high ISO setting.
  • Guided tours are available for a small tip. Guides provide rich context about the Quirino family and 19th-century Ilocano life.
  • Don't touch the exhibits. Many items are original and irreplaceable — stay behind barriers and ropes.
  • Dress respectfully. This is a historical landmark — avoid beachwear or overly casual attire.
Visitor Guide

How to Experience Syquia Mansion

A complete room-by-room guide to exploring the mansion, understanding its history, and combining it with nearby Vigan heritage sites.

Inside the Mansion
Step 1

Exterior & Facade

Before entering, study the facade. Notice the classic Ilocano-Chinese Mestizo design — stone ground floor, wooden upper story, wide capiz shell windows, and a red-tiled roof. The blue National Historical Commission marker at the entrance marks this as a National Historical Landmark.

Step 2

Ground Floor Exhibits

The stone-walled ground floor houses introductory exhibits about the Syquia family, Vigan's history, and the architectural style of the house. Read the timeline panels to understand the context before heading upstairs.

Step 3

Upper Floor — Living Quarters

Climb the original wooden staircase to the upper floor. The main living area is furnished with original 19th-century pieces — antique chairs, a dining table set for guests, and a sala (living room) that feels frozen in time. The capiz shell windows filter the light beautifully.

Step 4

President Quirino's Room

Visit the room where Elpidio Quirino was born in 1890. See his childhood belongings, family photographs, and personal items. A display traces his journey from this modest Vigan home to the presidency of the Philippines · a remarkable story of ambition and resilience.

Step 5

The Azotea & Garden

End your visit on the azotea — the open rear terrace that is a hallmark of Ilocano ancestral homes. It was used for drying laundry, socializing, and enjoying the evening breeze. A quiet, contemplative space that connects you to the daily life of the Syquia family.

Nearby in Vigan
3 min walk

Calle Crisologo

The iconic cobblestone street lined with ancestral houses. Best at dawn or dusk when the gas lamps glow and the crowds are thin. The most photographed street in Vigan.

5 min walk

Plaza Salcedo & Saint Paul Cathedral

The main plaza and the massive Baroque cathedral built in 1641. The dancing fountain show runs on weekend evenings. A perfect spot for sunset watching.

5 min walk

Crisologo Museum

The ancestral home of the Crisologo political family — another well-preserved heritage house turned museum. A natural companion to the Syquia Mansion visit.

10 min tricycle

Bantay Bell Tower

A 400-year-old colonial watchtower built in 1591. Climb to the top for a 360 panoramic view of Vigan and the Abra River — one of the best views in Ilocos Sur.
