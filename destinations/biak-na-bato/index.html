Introduction & History

Bulacan's Revolutionary Wilderness

Located in San Miguel, Bulacan, Biak-na-Bato National Park is a protected area of limestone caves, rivers, and forest that holds a unique place in Philippine history. In 1897, revolutionary leader Emilio Aguinaldo and the Katipunan forces established their mountain headquarters in the caves of Biak-na-Bato, using the rugged terrain as a natural fortress during the Philippine Revolution against Spanish colonial rule.

The Biak-na-Bato Pact was signed here in December 1897 — an agreement between Aguinaldo and the Spanish colonial government that temporarily ended hostilities. The caves and the surrounding landscape thus witnessed one of the most pivotal chapters of the Philippine independence movement. The park was declared a National Park in 1937 to protect both its natural and historical significance.

Today, the park offers a combination of historical exploration and natural adventure. The cave system includes multiple chambers with impressive formations, and the Madlum River that flows through the park is crystal clear and perfect for swimming. The surrounding forest is home to diverse wildlife and the trails offer rewarding hikes through the limestone karst landscape.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Biak-na-Bato

Biak-na-Bato National Park is located in San Miguel, Bulacan — about 80 km north of Manila. The nearest major city is Malolos, the capital of Bulacan.

01

Bus from Manila to San Miguel

Take a bus from Cubao or Monumento to San Miguel, Bulacan. Travel time is about 2₱2.5 hours. Alternatively, take a bus to Malolos and then a jeepney or van to San Miguel.

Travel time: ~2₱2.5 hours  |  ~₱100–₱150 one-way
02

San Miguel to the Park

From San Miguel town center, hire a tricycle or habal-habal to the Biak-na-Bato National Park entrance. The ride takes about 15₱20 minutes. The park entrance is well-signposted from the main road.

Tricycle to park: ~₱50–₱80  |  — ~15₱20 minutes
03

Register at the Park Office

Register at the DENR park office at the entrance and pay the entrance fee. Local guides are available and recommended for the cave exploration — they provide historical context and ensure your safety in the cave system.

Entrance fee: ~₱50–₱100  |  Guide: ~₱150–₱250
04

Combine with Bulacan Heritage Tour

Biak-na-Bato is best combined with Barasoain Church in Malolos for a full Bulacan history day. Start at the church in the morning, then head to the park for the afternoon cave and river experience.

Combine with Barasoain Church & Mount Balagbag  |  Full day recommended

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): Best time — lower river levels make the Madlum River safer and more enjoyable for swimming. Cave trails are drier and more accessible.
  • Morning visits: The caves are coolest and most atmospheric in the morning. Start early to explore before the afternoon heat.
  • Avoid heavy rain periods: Flash floods can make the river and cave trails dangerous. Check weather forecasts before visiting.
  • Weekdays: Fewer visitors and a more peaceful, contemplative atmosphere in the historically significant caves.
🔦

Cave & River Tips

  • Hire a local guide for the cave exploration — they provide historical context about the revolutionary period and ensure your safety.
  • Bring a flashlight or headlamp — cave lighting is minimal in the deeper sections.
  • Wear non-slip shoes for both the cave floors and the river rocks.
  • Swim in the Madlum River — the clear, cool water is perfect for swimming after the cave exploration.
  • Don't touch the formations — the stalactites and stalagmites are irreplaceable natural and historical features.
🎒

What to Pack

  • Headlamp or flashlight with fresh batteries.
  • Non-slip shoes suitable for both cave floors and river rocks.
  • Swimwear for the Madlum River.
  • Insect repellent — the forest trails have mosquitoes.
  • Packed lunch and water — food options inside the park are limited.
  • Cash — no ATMs in San Miguel. Withdraw in Malolos before heading out.
Visitor Guide

How to Experience Biak-na-Bato

A complete guide to the park and a suggested Bulacan day tour.

At the Park
Step 1

Historical Orientation

Begin with your guide's historical orientation — the story of Aguinaldo's mountain headquarters, the Katipunan forces, and the Biak-na-Bato Pact. Understanding the history transforms the cave exploration from a nature trip into a journey through Philippine revolutionary history.

Step 2

Cave Exploration

Explore the cave system with your guide. The chambers where Aguinaldo and his forces sheltered are among the most historically charged spaces in the Philippines. The limestone formations add natural drama to the historical significance.

Step 3

Madlum River Swimming

After the caves, head to the Madlum River for a swim in the crystal-clear water. The river flows through the park with cool, refreshing mountain water — a perfect contrast to the cave exploration.

Step 4

Forest Trail Walk

Walk the forest trails surrounding the cave system. The limestone karst landscape is beautiful and the forest is rich in birdlife. Your guide can point out the natural features and wildlife of the park.

Bulacan Day Tour
Morning
