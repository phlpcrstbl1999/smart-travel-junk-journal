Introduction & History

The Birthplace of Philippine Independence

Located in Kawit, Cavite, the Aguinaldo Shrine is the ancestral home of General Emilio Aguinaldo — the revolutionary leader who became the first President of the Philippines. The house is a magnificent example of late 19th-century Filipino architecture, featuring a distinctive facade with a central balcony that has become one of the most iconic images in Philippine history.

It was from this balcony that Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine Independence on June 12, 1898 — the first declaration of independence in Asia. The Philippine flag was unfurled for the first time, and the national anthem was played for the first time. This single moment transformed the house into one of the most sacred sites in the Philippines, and June 12 is now celebrated as Philippine Independence Day every year.

Today, the shrine is a National Cultural Treasure and a museum open to the public. The house has been meticulously preserved and contains original furniture, personal belongings, historical documents, and artifacts from Aguinaldo's life and the revolutionary period. Hidden rooms, secret passages, and a trapdoor — features built to help Aguinaldo evade capture — add a fascinating dimension to the tour.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Aguinaldo Shrine

Aguinaldo Shrine is located in Kawit, Cavite — about 30 km south of Manila. It's one of the most accessible heritage destinations from the capital.

01

Bus or Van from Manila to Kawit

Take a bus or van from Baclaran or Pasay to Kawit, Cavite. Travel time is about 1₱1.5 hours depending on traffic. Alternatively, take the LRT-1 to Baclaran and then a jeepney to Kawit.

Travel time: ~1₱1.5 hours  |  ~₱40–₱80 one-way
02

Tricycle to the Shrine

From Kawit town center, take a tricycle to the Aguinaldo Shrine. The shrine is located along the main road in Kawit and is well-signposted. The ride takes about 5₱10 minutes.

Tricycle: ~₱20–₱30  |  — ~5₱10 minutes
03

Drive from Manila

Driving from Manila via CAVITEX or Coastal Road takes about 45 minutes to 1 hour. Parking is available near the shrine. The drive through Cavite's coastal towns is scenic and pleasant.

Drive: ~45 min₱1 hour  |  Parking available near the shrine
04

Arrive at the Shrine

Entry to the shrine includes a guided tour of the house. Tours are conducted regularly and last about 45₱60 minutes. The guides are knowledgeable and bring the history of the house and the revolution to life.

Entrance fee: ~₱50–₱100  |  Open TueSun 8AM₱5PM

Essential Visitor Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • June 12 (Independence Day): The most meaningful day to visit — commemorative ceremonies, flag-raising, and cultural events at the shrine. Book early as crowds are large.
  • Morning (8:00₱11:00 AM): The shrine is peaceful and the guided tours are most informative in the morning before the afternoon crowds.
  • Year-round: The shrine is open Tuesday through Sunday throughout the year.
  • Weekdays: Fewer visitors and a more personal, contemplative atmosphere.
📌

What to See

  • The Independence Balcony: The iconic balcony from which Aguinaldo proclaimed independence on June 12, 1898. Standing here is a deeply moving experience.
  • The State Rooms: Original furniture, personal belongings, and period decor from Aguinaldo's era.
  • Hidden Rooms & Secret Passages: The house contains hidden rooms, a trapdoor, and secret passages built to help Aguinaldo evade capture.
  • The Museum Exhibits: Historical documents, photographs, weapons, and artifacts from the Philippine Revolution.
📌

Etiquette & Practical Tips

  • Join the guided tour. The guides provide essential historical context that makes the visit far more meaningful.
  • Dress respectfully. The shrine is a national memorial — avoid beachwear or overly casual attire.
  • Photography is allowed in most areas — check with your guide about any restricted sections.
  • Combine with Corregidor Island for a full Cavite heritage day — both sites are deeply connected to Philippine history.
Visitor Guide

How to Experience Aguinaldo Shrine

A complete guide to the shrine and a suggested Cavite heritage day tour.

At the Shrine
Step 1

The Facade & Grounds

Approach the shrine and study the magnificent facade — the central balcony, the ornate woodwork, and the architectural details that reflect the wealth and status of the Aguinaldo family in the late 19th century.

Step 2

The Guided Tour

Join the guided tour of the house interior — the state rooms, the personal quarters, the hidden rooms, and the secret passages. The guide will bring the history of the house and the revolution to life with stories and context.

Step 3

The Independence Balcony

Stand on the balcony from which Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine Independence on June 12, 1898. Look out over the town of Kawit and imagine the scene — the crowd below, the flag unfurling, the anthem playing for the first time.

Step 4

The Museum Exhibits

Explore the museum exhibits — historical documents, photographs, weapons, and artifacts from the Philippine Revolution and Aguinaldo's presidency. The collection is extensive and well-curated.

Cavite Day Tour
Morning

Aguinaldo Shrine

Start your Cavite day at the birthplace of Philippine independence. Allow 1.5₱2 hours for the guided tour and museum.

Midday

Corregidor Island

Take the ferry from Manila to Corregidor Island — the WWII fortress island at the mouth of Manila Bay. A full-day experience combining history and dramatic coastal scenery.

Afternoon

Tagaytay Ridge

Drive up to Tagaytay for the iconic view of Taal Volcano and Lake, fresh highland air, and excellent Cavite cuisine. A perfect scenic close to a heritage-rich day.
