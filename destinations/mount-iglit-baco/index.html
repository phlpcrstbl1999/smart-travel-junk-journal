Introduction

The Last Home of the Tamaraw

Mount Iglit–Baco National Park is a 75,445-hectare UNESCO World Heritage Site in Occidental Mindoro — the last remaining stronghold of the Tamaraw (Bubalus mindorensis), a critically endangered dwarf buffalo found only on Mindoro Island. With fewer than 600 individuals remaining in the wild, the Tamaraw is one of the rarest large mammals on Earth, and Mount Iglit–Baco is the only place where it can be seen in its natural habitat.

The park protects a dramatic landscape of montane grasslands, mossy forests, and rugged mountain terrain — the Iglit and Baco mountain ranges rise to over 2,000 meters above sea level, creating a diverse ecosystem that supports not only the Tamaraw but also the Mindoro Bleeding-heart Pigeon, the Mindoro Hornbill, and numerous other endemic species found nowhere else on Earth. The park is also home to the Mangyan people — the indigenous inhabitants of Mindoro — whose ancestral lands overlap with the park boundaries.

Visiting Mount Iglit–Baco requires advance coordination with the DENR (Department of Environment and Natural Resources) and the park management. Guided treks to the Tamaraw viewing areas are the primary visitor activity — the best time to see the Tamaraw is in the early morning and late afternoon when the animals graze in the open grasslands. Sightings are not guaranteed but are relatively reliable in the core viewing areas.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Mount Iglit–Baco

The park is accessed from San Jose, Occidental Mindoro — reached by flying from Manila or by overnight ferry from Batangas.

01

Fly to San Jose, Mindoro

Fly from Manila (NAIA) to San Jose Airport, Occidental Mindoro — about 1 hour. From San Jose, coordinate with the DENR or a local guide for transport to the park entrance at Siburan (about 1.5 hours by road).

Manila to San Jose: ~1 hour  |  San Jose to Siburan: ~1.5 hours
02

Coordinate with DENR in Advance

Visits to Mount Iglit–Baco require advance coordination with the DENR Occidental Mindoro office in San Jose. A mandatory park ranger guide is required for all treks. Permits and fees must be arranged before arrival at the park.

DENR San Jose: coordinate at least 1 week in advance  |  Ranger guide: mandatory
03

Trek to the Tamaraw Viewing Area

The main Tamaraw viewing area is in the Iglit grasslands — a 2₱4 hour trek from the park entrance depending on the route. The best viewing times are early morning (5₱8 AM) and late afternoon (4₱6 PM) when the Tamaraw graze in the open grasslands.

Trek: 2₱4 hours  |  Best viewing: 5₱8 AM & 4₱6 PM  |  Park fee required
04

Overnight Camping

For the best chance of seeing the Tamaraw, an overnight camping trip in the park is strongly recommended — allowing you to be at the viewing area at dawn and dusk. Basic camping facilities are available. Bring all food, water, and camping equipment.

🚌 Camping: basic facilities  |  Dawn viewing: best Tamaraw sightings

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Trek

📌

Seeing the Tamaraw

  • Early morning is essential: The Tamaraw is most active at dawn — be at the viewing area by 5₱6 AM for the best sightings. The animals graze in the open grasslands in the cool morning hours before retreating to the forest.
  • Sightings not guaranteed: The Tamaraw is a wild animal in a large park — sightings are relatively reliable in the core viewing areas but cannot be guaranteed. Patience and quiet are essential.
  • Keep your distance: The Tamaraw is a wild buffalo and can be dangerous if approached too closely. Always follow your ranger guide's instructions and maintain a safe distance.
  • Binoculars essential: Bring good binoculars — the Tamaraw may be visible at a distance across the grasslands. A telephoto lens is recommended for photography.
📌

The Park & Its Ecosystem

  • Montane grasslands: The Iglit grasslands are the primary habitat of the Tamaraw — vast open grasslands at altitude, surrounded by mossy forest. The landscape is dramatic and beautiful.
  • Endemic birds: The park is a birdwatcher's paradise — the Mindoro Bleeding-heart Pigeon, Mindoro Hornbill, Mindoro Racquet-tail Parrot, and numerous other Mindoro endemics can be seen in the forest areas.
  • Mangyan communities: The indigenous Mangyan people have lived in the Mindoro highlands for centuries. Respectful interaction with Mangyan communities near the park is possible — ask your guide for guidance.
  • Conservation significance: Every visit to the park contributes to the conservation of the Tamaraw. The park fees fund ranger salaries and anti-poaching patrols.
📌

What to Bring

  • Sturdy hiking boots: The terrain is rugged — proper hiking boots with ankle support are essential. The grassland trails can be slippery when wet.
  • Warm clothing: The park is at altitude — temperatures can drop significantly at night and in the early morning. Bring a fleece or light jacket even in the dry season.
  • Water and food: Bring all food and water for your stay — there are no shops or restaurants in the park. At least 2 liters of water per person per day.
  • Insect repellent: The grasslands and forest edges have mosquitoes and other insects. Bring effective repellent and consider long sleeves and trousers for the trek.
Mini Itinerary

How to Plan a Tamaraw Wildlife Trek

Two days in Mount Iglit–Baco — trekking to the grasslands, camping overnight, and watching the Tamaraw at dawn — is one of the most extraordinary wildlife experiences in the Philippines.

Day 1
Morning

Fly to San Jose & DENR Briefing

Fly to San Jose in the morning. Meet your DENR ranger guide and receive the park briefing. Drive to the Siburan park entrance and begin the trek to the camping area in the Iglit grasslands.

Afternoon

Trek to Grasslands & Camp Setup

Trek 2₱4 hours through the forest and into the Iglit grasslands. Set up camp before dark. In the late afternoon, take a first look at the viewing area — Tamaraw are sometimes visible at dusk.

Day 2
5:00 AM

Dawn Tamaraw Viewing

Wake before dawn and position yourself at the viewing area. As the sun rises over the Iglit grasslands, the Tamaraw emerge to graze — one of the most moving wildlife experiences in the Philippines. Fewer than 600 of these animals exist on Earth.

Morning

Birdwatching & Forest Walk

After the dawn viewing, explore the forest edges for endemic birds — the Mindoro Bleeding-heart Pigeon and Mindoro Hornbill are the most sought-after species. Your ranger guide will know the best spots.

Afternoon

Trek Out & Return to San Jose

Trek back to the park entrance and drive to San Jose. Combine with a visit to Pandan Island or Apo Reef if your schedule allows — San Jose is the gateway to both.
