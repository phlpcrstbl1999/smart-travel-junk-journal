Introduction & History

The Little Sahara of the Philippines

Stretching across more than 85 hectares along the coast of Ilocos Norte, the Paoay Sand Dunes are one of the most dramatic natural landscapes in the Philippines. Formed over thousands of years by wind-driven sand from the South China Sea, these towering dunes rise up to 30 meters high — earning the nickname "Little Sahara."

Located in the municipality of Paoay, the dunes sit just a few kilometers from the iconic Paoay Church — a UNESCO World Heritage Site built in 1694. The contrast of ancient Baroque architecture and sweeping desert sands makes this corner of Ilocos Norte unlike anywhere else in Southeast Asia.

Whether you're here to race across the dunes on a 4x4 vehicle, sandboard down steep slopes, or simply watch the sunset paint the sand in shades of amber and rose — Paoay delivers an adventure you won't forget.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to the Dunes

Paoay Sand Dunes is located in Paoay, Ilocos Norte, about 490 km north of Manila. You can fly or take an overnight bus — both are great options.

01

Fly Manila to Laoag

The fastest way is to fly from NAIA or Clark Airport to Laoag International Airport. Philippine Airlines and Cebu Pacific operate daily flights. Laoag is the capital of Ilocos Norte and just 20 km from Paoay.

Flight: ~1 hour  |  ~₱1,500₱3,500 one-way
02

Take an Overnight Bus from Manila

Partas, Farinas, and Florida Bus Lines operate overnight buses from Cubao or Pasay to Laoag City. It's a long but comfortable ride — book a deluxe or premium seat for the best experience.

Travel time: ~10₱12 hours  |  ~₱800–₱1,200 one-way
03

Laoag to Paoay Town

From Laoag, take a tricycle or jeepney to Paoay town (about 20₱30 minutes). You can also rent a tricycle for the whole day to cover the dunes, Paoay Church, and Lake Paoay in one trip.

Tricycle: ~₱150–₱200  |  — ~25 minutes
04

Arrive at the Sand Dunes Jump-Off

The main dunes entrance is at Brgy. Suba, Paoay. 4x4 operators and sandboard rentals are available right at the jump-off point. Book your 4x4 ride in advance on weekends — slots fill up fast.

Entrance fee: ~₱50  |  4x4 ride: ~₱1,500₱2,000

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • November — April (Dry Season): Best time — clear skies, firm sand, and perfect conditions for 4x4 rides and sandboarding.
  • December — February: Peak season with the most visitors. Book 4x4 rides and accommodation in advance.
  • Sunrise & Sunset: The dunes are most photogenic at golden hour — the sand glows amber and shadows create dramatic textures.
  • Avoid May — October: Rainy season makes the dunes wet and soft — 4x4 rides may be suspended.
🎒

What to Pack

  • Closed-toe shoes or sandals — sand gets extremely hot midday. Avoid flip-flops for 4x4 rides.
  • Sunscreen (SPF 50+) — zero shade on the dunes; UV exposure is intense.
  • Sunglasses & bandana — wind kicks up sand constantly.
  • Cash — no ATMs near the dunes. Withdraw in Laoag before heading out.
  • Change of clothes — you will be covered in sand. Bring a bag for sandy items.
  • Water (at least 1.5L) — it gets very hot; dehydration is a real risk.
⚠

Safety & Etiquette

  • Always use a licensed 4x4 operator. Unlicensed drivers skip safety briefings and take dangerous routes.
  • Wear your seatbelt on 4x4 rides — the terrain is rough and rolls are possible.
  • Don't go alone into the dunes. It's easy to get disoriented — always go with a guide or group.
  • Respect the dunes. Don't litter — the wind spreads trash across the entire landscape.
  • Visit Paoay Church respectfully — it is an active place of worship, not just a photo backdrop.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend 48 Hours in Paoay

Two days is the perfect amount of time to conquer the dunes, explore Ilocos heritage, and soak in the coastal scenery.

Day 1
6:00 AM

Sunrise at the Sand Dunes

Arrive early to beat the heat and the crowds. The dunes at sunrise are absolutely magical — golden light, long shadows, and cool sand underfoot. This is the best time for photography.

8:00 AM

4x4 Dune Bashing

Book a 4x4 ride at the jump-off point. Experienced drivers take you deep into the dunes — expect sharp turns, steep drops, and a lot of screaming. It's exhilarating and completely safe with a licensed operator.

10:00 AM

Sandboarding

Rent a sandboard (available at the jump-off) and try sliding down the dune slopes. No experience needed — guides will show you the technique. Expect to fall, laugh, and go again.

1:00 PM

Lunch in Paoay Town

Head to Paoay town for lunch. Try bagnet (Ilocano crispy pork belly), pinakbet, and empanada at a local carinderia. Ilocano food is some of the best in the Philippines.

3:00 PM

Paoay Church & Museum

Visit the Saint Augustine Church of Paoay — a UNESCO World Heritage Site built in 1694. The massive coral-stone buttresses are unlike any church in the world. Walk the grounds and visit the small museum inside.

5:30 PM

Sunset at Lake Paoay

End the day at Lake Paoay (Paoay Lake) — a serene freshwater lake just minutes from the church. Watch the sunset reflect off the water while local fishermen bring in their catch.

Day 2
7:00 AM

Drive to Laoag City

Head to Laoag City (20 km from Paoay) for a proper Ilocano breakfast. Try the famous Laoag empanada at the Laoag Empanada Park — a crispy, egg-filled street food unique to Ilocos Norte.

9:00 AM

Sinking Bell Tower & Laoag Cathedral

Visit the iconic Sinking Bell Tower — a 16th-century detached bell tower that has slowly sunk into the ground over centuries. The nearby Saint William Cathedral is equally impressive.

11:00 AM

Bangui Windmills

Drive 30 km north to the Bangui Windmills — a row of 20 giant wind turbines standing along the South China Sea coast. The contrast of white turbines, blue sea, and green hills is stunning and very photogenic.

2:00 PM

Pasalubong Shopping in Laoag

Stop at the Laoag Public Market or local pasalubong shops for Ilocano souvenirs — bagnet vacuum packs, pinipig cookies, Abel Iloko woven textiles, and basi wine.

4:00 PM

Head Back to Manila

Catch your flight from Laoag Airport or board your overnight bus back to Manila. Consider extending your trip to nearby Pagudpud (1.5 hours north) — home to some of the Philippines' most beautiful beaches.
