Introduction & History

The Temple of the Goddess of the Sea

Built in 1978 by the Chinese-Filipino community of La Union, the Ma-Cho Temple (also known as the Shrine of Ma-Cho Po) is dedicated to Mazu — the Chinese goddess of the sea, protector of sailors and fishermen. The temple sits on a hillside in Poro Point, San Fernando, La Union, overlooking the South China Sea.

The temple complex is a stunning display of traditional Chinese architecture — red and gold pagodas, ornate dragon sculptures, colorful ceramic tile work, and incense-filled prayer halls. The centerpiece is a 7-story pagoda tower that can be climbed for panoramic views of the coastline, the city of San Fernando, and on clear days, the distant mountains of the Cordillera range.

The temple is an active place of worship for the Chinese-Filipino community and draws thousands of devotees during major Chinese festivals — particularly Chinese New Year and the Feast of Ma-Cho in May. Visitors of all faiths are welcome to explore the grounds, light incense, and take in the stunning architecture and views.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Ma-Cho Temple

The Ma-Cho Temple is located at Poro Point, San Fernando, La Union — about 270 km north of Manila and easily combined with a San Juan surfing trip.

01

Bus from Manila to San Fernando

Take a bus from Cubao or Pasay to San Fernando, La Union. Partas, Dominion, and Philippine Rabbit operate this route. Travel time is about 5₱6 hours.

Travel time: ~5₱6 hours  |  ~₱400–₱600
02

Tricycle to Poro Point

From San Fernando city center or the bus terminal, hire a tricycle to Poro Point. Tell the driver you're going to the Ma-Cho Temple — it's well known locally. The ride takes about 15₱20 minutes.

Tricycle: ~₱80–₱120  |  — ~15₱20 minutes
03

From San Juan Surf Area

If you're already in San Juan for surfing, the Ma-Cho Temple is just 15₱20 minutes away by tricycle. It makes a perfect half-day side trip — visit the temple in the morning before your afternoon surf session.

From San Juan: ~₱100–₱150  |  — ~15₱20 minutes
04

Arrive at the Temple

The temple is located on a hill at Poro Point — you'll see the colorful pagoda from the road. There's a small parking area at the base. The entrance is free, but donations are appreciated. Open daily from early morning to evening.

Free entry  |  Open daily ~6:00 AM — 6:00 PM

Essential Visitor Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • Early Morning (7:00₱9:00 AM): The temple is peaceful and uncrowded. Morning light illuminates the red and gold architecture beautifully.
  • Chinese New Year (JanFeb): The temple is spectacularly decorated with lanterns and red banners. Expect large crowds of devotees and visitors.
  • Feast of Ma-Cho (May): The most important festival at the temple — colorful processions, offerings, and traditional ceremonies.
  • Avoid weekends during peak season (NovJan) when tour groups arrive. Weekday mornings are most peaceful.
📌

What to See & Do

  • Climb the 7-story pagoda for panoramic views of the South China Sea, San Fernando Bay, and the Cordillera mountains on clear days.
  • Explore the prayer halls — see the ornate altars, incense burners, and statues of Mazu and other deities.
  • Admire the dragon sculptures — the temple is adorned with intricate ceramic dragons, phoenixes, and mythological figures.
  • Light incense as an offering — incense sticks are available at the temple for a small donation.
  • Watch the sunset from the temple grounds — the view of the sun setting over the South China Sea is stunning.
📌

Etiquette & Respect

  • Dress modestly. Cover shoulders and knees when entering the prayer halls. The temple is an active place of worship.
  • Remove shoes before entering the main prayer hall — follow the signs and the example of other visitors.
  • Speak softly inside the temple. Be respectful of devotees who are praying.
  • Ask before photographing devotees during prayer or ceremonies.
  • No littering. The temple grounds are meticulously maintained — respect the space.
Visitor Guide

How to Experience Ma-Cho Temple

A complete guide to exploring the temple and combining it with nearby La Union attractions for a full day of discovery.

At the Temple
Step 1

Entrance & Grounds

Enter through the ornate main gate. Take a moment to appreciate the full scale of the complex — multiple prayer halls, pagoda towers, and garden areas spread across the hillside. Pick up incense sticks at the entrance if you'd like to make an offering.

Step 2

Main Prayer Hall

Visit the main prayer hall dedicated to Mazu. The altar is elaborately decorated with offerings of fruit, flowers, and incense. The smell of sandalwood incense and the sound of prayer bells create a deeply atmospheric experience.

Step 3

Climb the Pagoda Tower

Climb the 7-story pagoda for the best views in La Union. Each floor offers a wider panorama — by the top, you can see the entire coastline, San Fernando Bay, and the mountains beyond. Bring your camera.

Step 4

Explore the Dragon Sculptures

Walk around the temple grounds to admire the intricate ceramic sculptures — dragons, phoenixes, and scenes from Chinese mythology adorn every roofline and wall. The craftsmanship is extraordinary and highly photogenic.
