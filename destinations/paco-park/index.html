Introduction & History

A Cemetery Turned Garden of History

Paco Park (officially the Cemetery of San Fernando de Dilao) is a circular walled cemetery built in 1820 in the Paco district of Manila. It was the first municipal cemetery in the Philippines · a circular enclosure of niches and burial chambers surrounding a small chapel, designed in the neoclassical style.

The park's most significant historical connection is to Jos Rizal — after his execution at Bagumbayan on December 30, 1896, his body was secretly buried here by the Spanish authorities in an unmarked grave. His remains were later exhumed and transferred to the Rizal Monument in Luneta in 1912.

Today, Paco Park is a beautifully maintained public garden — the circular walls are covered in flowering vines, the chapel hosts weddings and concerts, and the shaded grounds are a peaceful retreat from the Manila heat. The park is famous for its Friday Concerts at the Park — free outdoor classical music concerts held in the garden.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

Getting to Paco Park

Paco Park is in the Paco district of Manila — easily reached by LRT or jeepney from Rizal Park and Intramuros.

01

LRT Line 1 — United Nations Station

Take LRT Line 1 to United Nations Station. Walk east along UN Avenue and then south along General Luna Street to reach Paco Park (~15 minutes).

LRT Line 1: UN Station  |  ~15 min walk
02

Jeepney from Intramuros

Take a jeepney from Intramuros heading toward Paco — the park is about 1.5 km southeast of Intramuros. Ask the driver to drop you at "Paco Park" on General Luna Street.

Jeepney from Intramuros: ~₱12–₱15  |  — ~10 min
03

Entrance & Hours

Paco Park is open daily. A small entrance fee applies. The circular garden, the chapel, and the burial niches are all accessible to visitors.

Entrance: ~₱50  |  Open daily: 8 AM₱5 PM
04

Friday Concerts at the Park

The Friday Concerts at the Park are free outdoor classical music concerts held in the Paco Park garden — one of the most atmospheric and affordable cultural experiences in Manila. Check the schedule before visiting.

Friday Concerts: free  |  Classical music in the garden

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

The Garden

  • The circular walls: The most distinctive feature of Paco Park — a circular enclosure of burial niches covered in flowering vines and tropical plants. Walk the perimeter for the full effect.
  • The chapel: The small neoclassical chapel at the center of the park is still used for weddings and special events. The interior is simple but beautiful.
  • Rizal's first burial: A marker indicates the approximate location where Rizal's body was secretly buried after his execution. A moving and historically significant spot.
  • The garden atmosphere: Paco Park is one of the most peaceful and atmospheric spaces in Manila — the combination of the old walls, the flowering plants, and the quiet garden is extraordinary.
📌

Friday Concerts

  • Free admission: The Friday Concerts at the Park are free — one of the best free cultural experiences in Manila.
  • Classical music: The concerts feature classical music performed by Filipino musicians — chamber music, solo recitals, and small ensemble performances.
  • Atmosphere: Listening to classical music in the circular garden of a 200-year-old cemetery at dusk is one of the most atmospheric experiences in Manila.
  • Check the schedule: The concert schedule varies — check the National Parks Development Committee website before visiting.
📌

Practical Tips

  • Best in the morning or late afternoon — the garden is most beautiful in the soft light of early morning or late afternoon.
  • Photography: Paco Park is one of the most photogenic spaces in Manila — the circular walls, the chapel, and the flowering vines make for beautiful images.
  • Combine with Intramuros: Paco Park is a short distance from Intramuros — combine both in a single morning for the complete historic Manila experience.
  • Weddings: The park is a popular wedding venue — you may encounter a wedding ceremony during your visit, especially on weekends.
Mini Itinerary

Historic Manila Morning

Combine Paco Park with Intramuros, Rizal Park, and the National Museum for the complete historic Manila experience.

Morning
8:00 AM

Paco Park

Start at Paco Park in the morning — the garden is most peaceful and beautiful before the midday heat. Walk the circular walls, visit the chapel, and find the Rizal burial marker.

10:00 AM

Intramuros

Walk or take a jeepney to Intramuros — Fort Santiago, San Agustin Church, and Manila Cathedral. The cobblestone streets are most atmospheric in the morning.

Afternoon
1:00 PM

Rizal Park & National Museum

Walk to Rizal Park and the National Museum of the Philippines. Free admission to the museum — the Spoliarium and the Tree of Life are unmissable.

5:30 PM

Manila Bay Sunset

End the day at the Manila Baywalk for the legendary sunset — the perfect finale to a day of historic Manila.
