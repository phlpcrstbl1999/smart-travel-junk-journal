Introduction & History

Zambales' Dramatic Island Sentinel

Located on Capones Island off the coast of San Antonio, Zambales, the Capones Island Lighthouse was built by the Spanish colonial government in 1890 to guide ships through the treacherous waters of the South China Sea at the northwestern tip of Luzon. The lighthouse stands on a rocky promontory at the island's highest point, visible from the Zambales coastline and from ships far out at sea.

Capones Island itself is a small, uninhabited island covered in dense tropical forest. The island is accessible only by boat from Pundaquit Beach — a 30₱40 minute crossing. From the landing point, a 30₱45 minute jungle trek through the island's forest leads to the lighthouse at the summit. The trail is steep and rocky in sections, but the views that open up as you ascend make every step worthwhile.

From the lighthouse, the panoramic view is extraordinary — the Zambales coastline stretching south, the South China Sea to the west, the distant outline of Anawangin and Nagsasa coves, and on clear days, the faint silhouette of the Bataan peninsula across the bay. The lighthouse itself is a beautiful 19th-century structure in good condition — a testament to Spanish colonial engineering that has guided ships for over 130 years.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Capones Island

Capones Island is located off the coast of San Antonio, Zambales — about 160 km northwest of Manila. The jump-off point is Pundaquit Beach.

01

Bus from Manila to San Antonio

Victory Liner operates buses from Cubao or Pasay to San Antonio, Zambales. Travel time is about 3₱4 hours. Alternatively, take a bus to Olongapo City and then a jeepney to San Antonio.

Travel time: ~3₱4 hours  |  ~₱200–₱300 one-way
02

San Antonio to Pundaquit Beach

From San Antonio town center, take a tricycle to Pundaquit Beach. Register at the barangay hall and pay the environmental fee. Negotiate a full island hopping package with your boatman covering Anawangin, Nagsasa, and Capones.

Tricycle to Pundaquit: ~₱50–₱80  |  Environmental fee: ~₱50–₱100
03

Boat to Capones Island

Capones Island is about 30₱40 minutes by boat from Pundaquit. The boat lands on the island's small beach, from where the jungle trek to the lighthouse begins. Your boatman will wait while you hike.

🚌 Boat to Capones: ~₱1,200₱1,800  |  — ~30₱40 minutes
04

Trek to the Lighthouse

From the landing beach, follow the trail through the jungle to the lighthouse — about 30₱45 minutes of hiking. The trail is steep in sections. Wear trekking shoes and bring water. The views from the top are worth every step.

Trek time: ~30₱45 min  |  Bring water  |  Wear trekking shoes

Essential Visitor Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): Best time — calm seas for the boat crossing and dry trails for the jungle trek.
  • Morning visits: Start the trek early to avoid the midday heat. The lighthouse at golden hour is spectacular.
  • Clear days: The panoramic views from the lighthouse are best on clear days — you can see the Bataan peninsula and the distant mountains of Luzon.
  • Avoid typhoon season (June — October): Rough seas make the boat crossing dangerous and the jungle trail slippery.
📌

The Jungle Trek

  • Wear trekking shoes with good grip — the trail is steep and rocky in sections. Sandals are not safe.
  • Bring plenty of water — the jungle trek is physically demanding and there are no water sources on the island.
  • Hire a local guide if available — they know the trail and can point out the island's wildlife and plants.
  • The trail is not well-marked in some sections — stay on the main path and follow your guide's directions.
  • Allow 1.5₱2 hours for the round trip trek including time at the lighthouse.
📷

Photography Tips

  • The lighthouse at golden hour is extraordinary — the warm light on the white tower against the blue sea is one of the most beautiful compositions in Zambales.
  • Shoot from the rocky promontory below the lighthouse for dramatic compositions with the sea in the foreground.
  • The view from the top — looking back at the Zambales coastline with Anawangin and Nagsasa visible — is the defining shot of the Capones experience.
  • Drone photography is spectacular here — check local regulations before flying.
Visitor Guide

How to Experience Capones Island

A complete guide to the lighthouse trek and the full Zambales cove island hopping experience.

At Capones
Step 1

Land on the Island Beach

The boat lands on Capones Island's small beach. Take a moment to appreciate the island setting — the dense jungle rising from the rocky shore, the clear water, and the dramatic coastal scenery. Your boatman will wait here while you trek.

Step 2

The Jungle Trek

Begin the 30₱45 minute trek through the island's tropical forest. The trail climbs steadily through dense vegetation — listen for birds and watch for monitor lizards. The forest is beautiful and the sound of the sea fades as you ascend.

Step 3

The Lighthouse

Emerge from the forest at the lighthouse. The 1890 Spanish colonial structure is impressive up close — well-maintained and still operational. Walk around the base and explore the rocky promontory for different perspectives.

Step 4

The Panoramic View

Take in the 360 panoramic view from the lighthouse promontory — the Zambales coastline, the South China Sea, the coves of Anawangin and Nagsasa, and the distant mountains of Luzon. One of the most dramatic viewpoints in Central Luzon.

Zambales Cove Hop
Morning

Anawangin Cove

Start at the iconic grey sand and pine tree cove. Swim, explore, and enjoy the morning atmosphere before the day-trippers arrive.

Midday

Nagsasa Cove

Continue to Nagsasa for the freshwater lagoon, wider beach, and fewer crowds. Have lunch and swim in the lagoon.

Afternoon

Capones Island Lighthouse

End your cove hop at Capones — jungle trek to the 1890 lighthouse and panoramic views before the boat back to Pundaquit.
