Introduction

The Philippines' Most Extraordinary Conservation Experience

Masungi Georeserve is a protected limestone karst landscape in Baras, Rizal — a 2,700-hectare conservation area within the Upper Marikina River Basin Protected Landscape, at the foot of the Sierra Madre mountain range. It is one of the most visually spectacular and intellectually compelling outdoor experiences in the Philippines · a place where conservation, art, and adventure intersect in a landscape of extraordinary geological beauty.

The reserve's signature Discovery Trail (2.5 km) winds through a series of dramatic limestone karst formations — towering rock spires, hidden caves, and ancient geological formations — connected by a system of hanging bridges, rope ladders, net hammocks, and bamboo walkways designed by the reserve's founders. The trail culminates at the Sapot — a giant net hammock suspended between limestone formations with views over the Sierra Madre forest canopy — one of the most photographed spots in the Philippines.

Masungi is not just a tourist attraction — it is a genuine conservation success story. The reserve was established in 2014 by the Masungi Georeserve Foundation to protect a critically threatened watershed from illegal quarrying and encroachment. The foundation has reforested thousands of hectares, restored wildlife corridors, and created a model for community-based conservation that has been recognized internationally. Every visit directly funds the conservation work.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Masungi Georeserve

Masungi Georeserve is in Baras, Rizal — about 50 km east of Manila along the MarikinaInfanta Highway (Marcos Highway). The drive takes about 1.5 hours.

01

Book Online — Weeks in Advance

Masungi strictly limits daily visitors to protect the ecosystem. Reservations are mandatory and must be made through the official Masungi Georeserve website (masungigeoreserve.com). Slots fill up weeks — sometimes months — in advance, especially for weekends. Book as early as possible.

Book at: masungigeoreserve.com  |  No walk-ins accepted
02

Drive via Marcos Highway

Drive via Marcos Highway (Marikina) — Baras, Rizal. The reserve entrance is along the MarikinaInfanta Highway. GPS coordinates are provided in your booking confirmation. The drive takes about 1.5 hours from Manila.

Manila to Masungi: ~1.5 hours  |  Parking at the reserve entrance
03

Discovery Trail (2.5 km)

The Discovery Trail is the main experience — a 2.5 km guided walk through the limestone karst landscape. The trail takes 3₱4 hours to complete. A mandatory guide accompanies each group. Wear closed-toe shoes with good grip — the trail involves rope ladders, hanging bridges, and uneven terrain.

Fee: ~₱1,500–₱2,000 per person  |  Trail: 3₱4 hours  |  Guide included
04

Legacy Trail (Advanced)

For more experienced hikers, the Legacy Trail (7.5 km) offers a deeper exploration of the reserve — longer, more challenging, and with even more dramatic limestone formations and forest views. Also requires advance booking and is limited to smaller groups.

Legacy Trail: 7.5 km  |  ~6₱7 hours  |  Moderate-challenging difficulty

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Booking & Preparation

  • Book as early as possible: Masungi is one of the most in-demand outdoor experiences in the Philippines. Weekend slots fill up months in advance. Weekday slots are easier to get but still require advance booking.
  • No walk-ins: The reserve strictly enforces its reservation policy. Arriving without a booking will result in being turned away — no exceptions.
  • Cancellation policy: Check the cancellation and rescheduling policy carefully when booking. Slots are non-transferable and refund policies are strict.
  • Group size limits: Groups are limited in size to minimize impact on the ecosystem. Large groups may need to book multiple slots on different days.
📌

What to Wear & Bring

  • Closed-toe shoes with grip: Mandatory. The trail involves rope ladders, hanging bridges, and rocky terrain. Sandals, flip-flops, and heels are not permitted.
  • Comfortable, flexible clothing: You'll be climbing, crawling through rock formations, and crossing hanging bridges. Wear clothes you can move freely in.
  • Water: Bring at least 1.5 liters per person. The trail is physically demanding and there are no water sources along the route.
  • No single-use plastics: Masungi has a strict no single-use plastic policy. Bring a reusable water bottle and avoid bringing plastic packaging into the reserve.
📷

Photography & Highlights

  • The Sapot: The giant net hammock suspended between limestone formations — the most iconic spot in the reserve and one of the most photographed outdoor locations in the Philippines. Allow time here for photos.
  • Tatay: A massive limestone formation that towers over the trail — the geological centerpiece of the Discovery Trail.
  • Hanging bridges: Several suspension bridges cross the limestone formations — dramatic photo opportunities with the Sierra Madre forest canopy below.
  • Golden hour: The late afternoon light on the limestone formations is extraordinary — if your slot allows, time your arrival at the Sapot for the late afternoon.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend a Day at Masungi & Rizal

Combine Masungi with Daranak Falls for the ultimate Rizal nature day — waterfalls in the morning, limestone karst in the afternoon.

Morning
6:00 AM

Depart Manila Early

Leave Manila by 6 AM. Drive via Marcos Highway to Tanay for Daranak Falls first — the morning is the best time for swimming in the natural pool before the crowds arrive.

8:00 AM

Daranak Falls — Morning Swim

Swim in the crystal-clear natural pool at Daranak Falls. Walk upstream to Batlag Falls. Have a picnic lunch at the eco-park before heading to Masungi.

Afternoon
1:30 PM

Arrive at Masungi Georeserve

Drive 30 minutes from Daranak to Masungi. Check in at the entrance with your booking confirmation. Meet your guide and begin the Discovery Trail briefing.

2:00 PM

Discovery Trail

The 2.5 km guided trail through the limestone karst landscape — rope ladders, hanging bridges, the Tatay formation, and the Sapot net hammock. Allow 3₱4 hours for the full experience.

6:00 PM

Sunset Dinner in Antipolo

Drive to Antipolo for dinner at a hilltop restaurant with views of Metro Manila. Padi's Point or Crescent Moon Caf are the classic choices — the perfect end to a full Rizal day.
