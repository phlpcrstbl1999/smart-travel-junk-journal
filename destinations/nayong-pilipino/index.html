Introduction

A Cultural Journey Through the Archipelago

Located within the Clark Freeport Zone in Angeles City, Pampanga, Nayong Pilipino Clark is a cultural theme park that celebrates the diversity and richness of Philippine heritage. The park features miniature replicas of the country's most iconic landmarks and heritage structures — from the Banaue Rice Terraces to the Mayon Volcano, from Intramuros to the Chocolate Hills of Bohol.

The park is organized into regional zones, each representing a different part of the Philippine archipelago — Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao. Within each zone, visitors can explore scaled-down versions of famous landmarks, traditional houses, and cultural artifacts that represent the unique heritage of each region. The park also features cultural performances, craft demonstrations, and traditional food from different Philippine regions.

Nayong Pilipino Clark is particularly valuable as an educational destination — it provides an accessible overview of Philippine cultural diversity that is especially meaningful for children and visitors who may not have the opportunity to travel to every corner of the archipelago. It is also a popular venue for cultural events, festivals, and school field trips throughout the year.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Nayong Pilipino Clark

Nayong Pilipino Clark is located inside the Clark Freeport Zone in Angeles City, Pampanga — about 80 km north of Manila.

01

Bus from Manila to Angeles / Clark

Genesis Transport, Victory Liner, and other bus lines operate services from Cubao or Pasay to Angeles City or directly to the Clark Freeport Zone. Travel time is about 1.5₱2 hours via NLEX and SCTEX.

Travel time: ~1.5₱2 hours  |  ~₱100–₱180 one-way
02

Enter Clark Freeport Zone

The Clark Freeport Zone has multiple entry gates. Visitors entering by private vehicle may need to register at the gate. By public transport, buses and jeepneys drop off at the Clark main gate, from where tricycles can take you to Nayong Pilipino.

Tricycle within Clark: ~₱50–₱80  |  Bring valid ID for gate registration
03

Arrive at Nayong Pilipino

Nayong Pilipino Clark is well-signposted within the Clark Freeport Zone. The park has a dedicated entrance with ticketing, parking, and visitor facilities. Check the park's schedule for cultural performances and special events before your visit.

Entrance fee: ~₱100–₱200  |  Open daily 8AM₱6PM
04

Combine with Pampanga Day Tour

Nayong Pilipino is best combined with the Bacolor Sunken Church for a full Pampanga cultural day. Start at the sunken church in the morning, then head to Clark for the afternoon cultural park experience.

Combine with Bacolor Church  |  Full Pampanga day recommended

Essential Visitor Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Visit

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • Weekday mornings: The park is least crowded on weekday mornings. You can explore the replicas and zones at your own pace without the weekend crowds.
  • Cultural performance schedules: Check the park's schedule for cultural shows, folk dance performances, and craft demonstrations — these are the highlights of a Nayong Pilipino visit.
  • School holidays: The park is busiest during school holidays and long weekends. Book tickets in advance if visiting during these periods.
  • Year-round: The park is open throughout the year. The outdoor exhibits are best enjoyed in dry season (NovemberMay).
—

What to See & Do

  • Regional zones: Walk through the Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao zones to see miniature replicas of iconic landmarks from each region.
  • Cultural performances: Watch folk dance performances representing different Philippine regional traditions — from the Igorot dances of the Cordillera to the Singkil of Mindanao.
  • Craft demonstrations: See artisans demonstrating traditional crafts — weaving, pottery, woodcarving, and other indigenous skills.
  • Regional food: Sample traditional dishes from different Philippine regions at the park's food stalls and restaurants.
📌

Practical Tips

  • Wear comfortable walking shoes — the park is large and involves significant walking between zones.
  • Bring sunscreen and a hat — many of the exhibits are outdoors and exposed to the sun.
  • Allow at least 3₱4 hours for a thorough visit — rushing through the park does the cultural content a disservice.
  • Buy local crafts and souvenirs at the park — your purchases support the artisans and cultural communities represented.
  • Bring cash — some vendors and activities within the park may be cash-only.
Visitor Guide

How to Experience Nayong Pilipino Clark

A complete guide to the cultural park and a suggested Pampanga day tour.

At the Park
Step 1

Luzon Zone

Begin with the Luzon zone — miniature replicas of the Banaue Rice Terraces, Mayon Volcano, Intramuros, and other iconic Luzon landmarks. The scale models are detailed and educational, providing a visual overview of Luzon's diverse heritage.

Step 2

Visayas Zone

Move to the Visayas zone — replicas of the Chocolate Hills of Bohol, the Magellan's Cross of Cebu, the Basilica del Santo Nio, and other Visayan landmarks. The zone also features traditional Visayan houses and cultural artifacts.

Step 3

Mindanao Zone

Explore the Mindanao zone — replicas of the Maranao royal houses, the Lanao del Sur mosque, and other Mindanao cultural landmarks. The zone highlights the rich Islamic and indigenous cultural heritage of the southern Philippines.

Step 4

Cultural Performance

Catch a scheduled cultural performance — folk dances, music, and traditional ceremonies from different Philippine regions. The performances are the most vivid and memorable part of the Nayong Pilipino experience.

Pampanga Day Tour
Morning

Bacolor Sunken Church

Start your Pampanga day at the sunken church — exterior walk, interior visit, bell tower, and Pinatubo Museum. A powerful and moving heritage experience.

Afternoon

Nayong Pilipino Clark

Head to Clark for the cultural park — regional zones, miniature replicas, cultural performances, and regional food. Allow 3₱4 hours for a thorough visit.

Evening

Kapampangan Dinner

End your day with dinner at a Kapampangan restaurant in Angeles City or San Fernando. Pampanga is considered the culinary capital of the Philippines · the food alone is worth the trip.
