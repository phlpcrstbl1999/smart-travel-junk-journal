Introduction & Overview

Cool Air, Pine Trees & Mountain Culture

Baguio City is the Summer Capital of the Philippines — a highland city in Benguet province in the Cordillera Administrative Region, sitting at 1,500 meters above sea level. Its cool climate (averaging 15₱23°C year-round), pine-forested hills, and mountain scenery have made it the most popular domestic travel destination in the Philippines for over a century.

Founded as a hill station by the American colonial administration in 1900, Baguio has grown into a vibrant city of over 300,000 people while retaining its distinctive highland character. It is known for its arts and crafts scene, the Panagbenga Flower Festival (February), the Burnham Park lake, the Mines View Park, the Session Road commercial district, and the surrounding strawberry farms of La Trinidad.

Baguio is also the gateway to the wider Cordillera — the Banaue Rice Terraces, Sagada, Kalinga, and Mt. Pulag are all accessible from the city.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Baguio City

Baguio is about 250 km north of Manila — a 5₱6 hour bus journey through the Cordillera foothills, or a short flight to nearby airports.

01

Bus from Manila (Most Popular)

Take a bus from Cubao, Pasay, or Sampaloc in Manila to Baguio City. Victory Liner, Genesis, and Partas are the main operators. The journey takes 5₱6 hours via NLEX and TPLEX. Buses run 24 hours.

Bus: ~5₱6 hrs  |  ~₱400–₱600 one-way
02

Drive from Manila

Drive via NLEX — TPLEX — Marcos Highway to Baguio. The drive takes 4₱5 hours in light traffic. The Marcos Highway section offers spectacular mountain scenery as you ascend into the Cordillera.

Drive: ~4₱5 hrs  |  Via NLEX + TPLEX + Marcos Hwy
03

Getting Around Baguio

Baguio has an extensive jeepney network covering all major attractions. Taxis and ride-hailing apps (Grab) are also available. Many attractions in the city center are walkable from Session Road.

Jeepney: ~₱12–₱15  |  Taxi: ~₱50–₱150
04

Best Areas to Stay

Stay near Session Road for the most central location — walking distance to Burnham Park, the public market, and the main commercial district. Book well in advance during peak season (December, Holy Week, Panagbenga Festival in February).

Book early for peak season  |  Peak: Dec, Feb, Holy Week

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • February (Panagbenga Festival): The Flower Festival transforms Baguio with elaborate floral floats, street dancing, and the city at its most festive. Book accommodation months in advance.
  • March — May (Summer): Peak domestic tourism season — Baguio is the escape from Manila's heat. Expect crowds and higher prices.
  • November — January: Coolest temperatures (can drop to 10°C at night). Bring a jacket. Christmas in Baguio is magical.
  • Avoid Holy Week and long weekends — traffic into Baguio can be severe. The Kennon Road and Marcos Highway become heavily congested.
📌

Top Experiences

  • Burnham Park: The city's central park with a boating lake, rose garden, and cycling paths — the heart of Baguio's public life.
  • La Trinidad Strawberry Farm: Pick fresh strawberries directly from the farm in the neighboring municipality of La Trinidad (~15 minutes from Baguio).
  • Baguio Public Market: The most vibrant market in the Cordillera — fresh vegetables, strawberry jam, ube jam, woven crafts, and local food.
  • Mines View Park: Panoramic views of the Cordillera mountains and the old gold and copper mines of Benguet.
📌

Baguio Food & Culture

  • Strawberry taho: The Baguio version of the classic Filipino snack — warm silken tofu with strawberry syrup instead of the usual arnibal. A must-try.
  • Pinikpikan: A traditional Cordillera chicken dish — try it at a local restaurant for an authentic highland food experience.
  • Ube jam and strawberry jam: The most popular pasalubong from Baguio — buy them at the public market for the best prices.
  • Session Road dining: The main commercial street has dozens of restaurants, cafes, and bakeries — perfect for a leisurely meal with mountain views.
Mini Itinerary

2 Days in Baguio City

Combine Baguio's city highlights with La Trinidad Strawberry Farm and a day trip to the surrounding Benguet highlands.

Day 1 — City
Morning

Burnham Park & City Center

Start at Burnham Park for a morning walk or boat ride on the lake. Then explore Session Road — browse the shops, have breakfast at a local cafe, and soak in the cool mountain air.

Afternoon

Baguio Public Market & Mines View

Spend the afternoon at the Baguio Public Market — buy strawberry jam, ube products, and woven crafts. Then head to Mines View Park for panoramic Cordillera views.

Evening

Dinner on Session Road

Have dinner at one of the many restaurants on Session Road. Try pinikpikan or etag (smoked Cordillera pork) at a local highland restaurant.

Day 2 — Highlands
Morning

La Trinidad Strawberry Farm

Take a jeepney to La Trinidad (~15 minutes) and pick fresh strawberries at the farm. The strawberry fields are most productive from November to May.

Afternoon

Camp John Hay & The Mansion

Visit Camp John Hay — the former American military rest and recreation facility, now a forest park with pine trees, golf course, and heritage buildings. Then see The Mansion, the official summer residence of the Philippine President.

Evening

Strawberry Taho & Departure

Have a final cup of strawberry taho from a street vendor before heading back to Manila. Buy your pasalubong — ube jam, strawberry jam, and Baguio crafts — at the public market.
