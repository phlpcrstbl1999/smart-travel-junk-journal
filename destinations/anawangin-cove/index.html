Introduction

Zambales' Most Iconic Cove

Located in San Antonio, Zambales, Anawangin Cove is one of the most photographed and most beloved natural destinations near Metro Manila. The cove is famous for its extraordinary combination of grey volcanic sand — a legacy of the 1991 Mount Pinatubo eruption — and a dense forest of Agoho pine trees that grow right up to the shoreline, creating a landscape unlike any other beach in the Philippines.

The cove is accessible only by boat from the jump-off point at Pundaquit Beach in San Antonio — a 20₱30 minute boat ride that adds to the sense of adventure and discovery. There are no permanent structures inside the cove — no hotels, no restaurants, no electricity. Visitors camp overnight in tents under the pine trees, cook their own food, and experience the cove in its most natural state.

Anawangin is best experienced as an overnight camping trip — arriving in the afternoon, watching the sunset over the South China Sea, sleeping under the stars, and waking up to the sunrise over the cove before the day-trippers arrive. The combination of the unique landscape, the boat journey, the camping experience, and the natural beauty makes Anawangin one of the most memorable destinations near Manila.

🏴 Boat Access Only  ·  San Antonio, Zambales
How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Anawangin Cove

Anawangin Cove is located in San Antonio, Zambales — about 160 km northwest of Manila. The jump-off point is Pundaquit Beach in San Antonio.

01

Bus from Manila to San Antonio

Victory Liner operates buses from Cubao or Pasay to San Antonio, Zambales. Travel time is about 3₱4 hours. Alternatively, take a bus to Olongapo City and then a jeepney to San Antonio.

Travel time: ~3₱4 hours  |  ~₱200–₱300 one-way
02

San Antonio to Pundaquit Beach

From San Antonio town center, take a tricycle to Pundaquit Beach — the jump-off point for Anawangin Cove. The ride takes about 15₱20 minutes. Register at the barangay hall and pay the environmental fee.

Tricycle to Pundaquit: ~₱50–₱80  |  — ~15₱20 minutes
03

Boat from Pundaquit to Anawangin

Hire a motorized banca boat at Pundaquit Beach for the 20₱30 minute crossing to Anawangin Cove. Negotiate the fare before boarding. The boat can also take you to Nagsasa Cove and Capones Island on the same trip.

🚌 Boat to Anawangin: ~₱1,000₱1,500  |  — ~20₱30 minutes
04

Set Up Camp

Arrive at the cove and set up your tent under the pine trees. Tent rentals are available at Pundaquit or inside the cove. Bring all your food, water, and supplies — there are no stores inside Anawangin. Leave no trace when you depart.

🚌 Tent rental: ~₱300–₱500  |  Environmental fee: ~₱50–₱100

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

📌

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): Best time — calm seas, clear skies, and the most comfortable camping conditions.
  • Overnight camping: The best way to experience Anawangin — arrive in the afternoon, watch the sunset, sleep under the stars, and wake up to the sunrise before day-trippers arrive.
  • Weekdays: Far fewer visitors. The cove feels more private and the pine forest atmosphere is more magical without crowds.
  • Avoid typhoon season (June — October): Rough seas can make the boat crossing dangerous and camping uncomfortable.
—

Camping Tips

  • Bring all your supplies — food, water, cooking equipment, and camping gear. There are no stores inside the cove.
  • Rent a tent at Pundaquit if you don't have your own — several vendors offer tent rentals with basic equipment.
  • Leave no trace. Pack out all your trash — the cove's beauty depends on every visitor's responsibility.
  • Bring a headlamp — there is no electricity in the cove. Nights are dark and beautiful.
  • Respect the pine trees. Do not cut branches or damage the Agoho pines — they are the defining feature of the cove.
🎒

What to Pack

  • Tent and sleeping bag — or rent at Pundaquit.
  • Food and water for the entire stay — at least 2L of water per person per day.
  • Cooking equipment — portable stove, pots, utensils.
  • Swimwear and water shoes — the cove is perfect for swimming.
  • Insect repellent — the pine forest has mosquitoes, especially at dusk.
  • Cash — for boat hire, environmental fees, and tent rental.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend 48 Hours in Anawangin

Two days is the perfect amount of time to experience Anawangin, explore Nagsasa Cove, and visit Capones Island Lighthouse.

Day 1
2:00 PM

Arrive at Anawangin

Take the afternoon boat from Pundaquit to Anawangin. Set up your tent under the pine trees and explore the cove before the light fades. The afternoon light on the grey sand and pine forest is extraordinary.

5:30 PM

Sunset Over the South China Sea

Watch the sunset from the beach. The sun sets directly over the South China Sea from Anawangin — the combination of the grey sand, the pine silhouettes, and the blazing sky is one of the most beautiful sunsets near Manila.

Evening

Campfire & Stargazing

Cook dinner over a campfire, enjoy the sounds of the sea and the pine forest, and stargaze away from city lights. The Milky Way is visible on clear nights. This is what Anawangin is all about.

Day 2
5:30 AM

Sunrise at Anawangin

Wake up for sunrise — the cove is magical in the early morning before other visitors arrive. The mist in the pine forest, the calm sea, and the golden light make for extraordinary photography.

9:00 AM

Boat to Nagsasa Cove

Ask your boatman to take you to Nagsasa Cove — a larger, equally beautiful cove about 20 minutes away. Nagsasa has a freshwater lagoon, a wider beach, and fewer visitors than Anawangin.

11:00 AM

Capones Island Lighthouse

Continue to Capones Island to visit the 19th-century Spanish lighthouse. The hike to the lighthouse takes about 30 minutes through the island's forest and offers dramatic views of the Zambales coastline.

2:00 PM

Return to Pundaquit

Board your boat back to Pundaquit and begin the journey home to Manila. You'll leave with grey sand in your shoes, pine resin on your fingers, and a strong urge to come back.
