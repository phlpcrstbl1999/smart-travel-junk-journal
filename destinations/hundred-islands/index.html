Introduction & History

The Philippines' Oldest National Park

Declared a National Park in 1940, Hundred Islands is one of the oldest protected areas in the Philippines. Located in Alaminos City, Pangasinan, the park encompasses 124 islands and islets scattered across 16 square kilometers of the Lingayen Gulf. Despite the name, there are actually 124 islands — not a hundred — but the name has stuck for over a century.

The islands are formed from ancient coral reefs that were uplifted over millions of years, creating dramatic limestone formations, sea caves, hidden lagoons, and white sand beaches. Each island has its own character — some are forested, some are bare rock, some have beaches, and some hide caves and tunnels accessible only by swimming.

The park is a haven for island hopping, snorkeling, kayaking, cliff jumping, and cave exploration. Three main islands — Governor's Island, Children's Island, and Quezon Island — have basic facilities and are the most visited. But the real magic lies in the smaller, less-visited islands where you can have an entire beach to yourself.

How to Get There
Location & Getting There

From Manila to Hundred Islands

Hundred Islands is located in Alaminos City, Pangasinan, about 250 km north of Manila. The jump-off point is the Lucap Wharf in Alaminos.

01

Bus from Manila to Alaminos

Five Star Bus and Dagupan Bus operate direct trips from Cubao or Pasay to Alaminos City, Pangasinan. Travel time is about 5₱6 hours. Buses run throughout the day and early morning.

Travel time: ~5₱6 hours  |  ~₱350–₱500 one-way
02

Tricycle to Lucap Wharf

From the Alaminos bus terminal, take a tricycle to Lucap Wharf — the main jump-off point for Hundred Islands. The ride takes about 10₱15 minutes. The wharf is where you register, pay fees, and hire your boat.

Tricycle: ~₱30–₱50  |  — ~10₱15 minutes
03

Register & Pay Fees at Lucap Wharf

At the wharf, register at the Hundred Islands National Park office and pay the entrance fee. Then hire a motorized banca boat for your island hopping tour. Negotiate the route and price with your boatman.

Entrance fee: ~₱50–₱100  |  — Boat rental: ~₱800–₱1,500/day
04

Start Your Island Hopping

Your boatman will take you to your chosen islands. Most tours cover 3₱5 islands in a half-day trip. For a full experience, book a full-day tour and ask your boatman to include some of the smaller, less-visited islands.

🚌 Half-day tour: ~3₱4 hours  |  Full-day: ~6₱7 hours

Essential Travel Tips

Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

🌞

Best Time to Visit

  • November — May (Dry Season): Best time — calm seas, clear water, and sunny skies. Ideal for snorkeling, kayaking, and cliff jumping.
  • March — May: Peak season with the most visitors. Book accommodation in Alaminos in advance, especially on weekends.
  • Weekdays: Far fewer crowds. The islands feel more private and the water is cleaner without the weekend rush.
  • Avoid June — October: Typhoon season brings rough seas and strong winds. Some islands may be inaccessible.
📌

Island Hopping Tips

  • Negotiate your boat route before departing. Tell your boatman which islands you want to visit — including smaller, less-visited ones.
  • Bring your own snorkeling gear if possible — rental gear at the wharf is available but quality varies.
  • Try cliff jumping at Quezon Island — there are platforms at various heights for different comfort levels.
  • Explore the caves on Governor's Island and other islands — some require swimming through tunnels to access hidden chambers.
  • Bring cash — there are no ATMs on the islands. Withdraw in Alaminos before heading to the wharf.
🎒

What to Pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen — you'll be in the sun all day. Protect your skin and the coral reefs.
  • Waterproof bag or dry bag — keep your phone, camera, and valuables dry on the boat.
  • Water shoes — rocky shorelines and coral make bare feet uncomfortable on some islands.
  • Packed lunch and water — food options on the islands are limited and expensive. Pack your own for a better experience.
  • Snorkeling mask and fins — the underwater world around the islands is beautiful and worth exploring.
Mini Itinerary

How to Spend 48 Hours in Hundred Islands

Two days gives you time to explore the main islands, discover hidden spots, and experience the best of Pangasinan.

Day 1
7:00 AM

Arrive at Lucap Wharf

Register at the park office, pay your entrance fee, and hire your banca boat. Negotiate a full-day tour covering at least 5₱6 islands. Depart early to beat the crowds and get the best light for photography.

8:00 AM

Governor's Island

Start at Governor's Island — the largest and most developed island. Explore the caves, climb the viewing deck for a panoramic view of the archipelago, and snorkel around the coral formations.

10:00 AM

Quezon Island & Cliff Jumping

Head to Quezon Island for cliff jumping — platforms at 5, 8, and 12 meters high. The water is deep and clear. Even if you don't jump, the island has a beautiful beach and good snorkeling.

12:00 PM

Lunch on Children's Island

Children's Island has the best beach in the park — wide, white sand, and calm shallow water. Have your packed lunch here, swim, and relax before the afternoon island hopping.

2:00 PM

Explore Smaller Islands

Ask your boatman to take you to some of the smaller, less-visited islands. Look for hidden lagoons, sea caves, and secluded beaches where you might be the only visitors. These are the real gems of the park.

5:00 PM

Sunset from the Boat

Ask your boatman to time your return to Lucap Wharf for sunset. Watching the sun set over the islands from the water is one of the most beautiful experiences in Pangasinan.

Day 2
7:00 AM

Kayaking at Sunrise

Rent a kayak at Lucap Wharf and paddle out to the nearest islands at sunrise. The calm morning water and golden light make for an unforgettable experience. Kayaks can be rented by the hour.

10:00 AM

Drive to Bolinao

Head west to Bolinao — about 1 hour from Alaminos. Visit Patar Beach for some of the most dramatic coastal scenery in Pangasinan, and Bolinao Falls for a refreshing swim in natural pools.

3:00 PM

Head Back to Manila

Catch an afternoon bus from Alaminos back to Manila. The journey takes about 5₱6 hours. Consider stopping in Dagupan City for the famous Pangasinan bangus (milkfish) before heading home.
